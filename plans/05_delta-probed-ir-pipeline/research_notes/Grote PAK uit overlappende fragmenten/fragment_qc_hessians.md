# Fragment-based quantum-chemical Hessians and vibrational spectra of large molecules, with attention to PAHs, graphene flakes and nanographenes

Search date: 11 November 2026. Tools: web search, Europe PMC REST API (abstracts and forward-citation lists), full text of two open PDFs (Collins 2014, Herbert 2019). Most ACS/AIP/ScienceDirect pages returned HTTP 403, so many numbers come from abstracts only; this is flagged where it matters.

## Claim test: has a large PAH's Hessian / vibrational correction been assembled from overlapping, corpus-size fragments?

### Takeaway
I found no published study that builds the Hessian, harmonic or anharmonic frequencies, or IR spectrum of a fused PAH or nanographene (34–60+ atoms) from overlapping PAH fragments of about 10–30 atoms and checks the result against a full calculation. The parts of the idea do exist separately, though. (a) Overlapping-fragment Hessians and IR/Raman/VCD/ROA spectra are well established (MTA 2008, GEBF 2008, FMO 2013, SMFA 2014, MIM 2015, Cartesian tensor transfer since 1997), but they were tested on peptides, proteins, water clusters, carbohydrates and molecular crystals. (b) Fragmenting PAHs while keeping aromatic sextets intact, using naphthalene/biphenyl-sized pieces (18–20 atoms), is published for *energies only* (AroBOROS 2020, AROFRAG 2024). (c) "High level on the fragments, low level on the whole molecule" vibrational corrections (MTA grafting, MIM2) are published, but not for PAHs. (d) The nearest competitor is the Barone group's 2026 work: transferable local vibrational corrections assessed on PAHs, and multilevel fragmentation for VPT2. So the claim holds only in a narrowed form, and it should be reworded (see Inferences).

### Cited Findings
- AroBOROS (Noffke, Beckett, Li, Raghavachari, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 16, 2160–2171, 2020, DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.9b00566) fragments PAHs within MIM into "biphenyl and naphthalene subsystems overlapping by whole sextet rings, ensuring all calculations are performed on aromatic molecules". Errors fall below chemical accuracy when the subsystems follow Clar's sextet structures. Test sets span 18–84 C, with larger PAHs and a nanotube fragment up to 132 C. The paper is about **energies**, not Hessians. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.9b00566%22)
- Europe PMC lists 6 citing papers for AroBOROS (AROFRAG ×2, strong-acid dissociation, Chan & Karton PAH thermochemistry, two Mayhall cluster-CI papers). None computes Hessians or vibrational spectra. — [Europe PMC citations API, MED/32119545](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/32119545/citations?format=json)
- AROFRAG (Masoumifeshani & Korona, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 20, 1078–1095, 2024, DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.3c00875) treats "a sextet ring as a single unbreakable unit". It uses naphthalene/biphenyl and larger PAH subsystems with weights, is combined with MIM, and is applied to graphene structures, B/N-doped fullerenes, nanotubes and fullerenes. It reaches "a few milli-hartree accuracy" in **energies** at a fraction of the cost. Four citing papers, none vibrational (COMPAS-3 dataset, density-based MBE for peptides, AROFRAG interaction energies, a 2026 π-hydrocarbon chemical-space paper). — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.3c00875%22); [citations MED/38252847](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/38252847/citations?format=json)
- Yeole & Gadre (J. Chem. Phys. 132, 094102, 2010, DOI 10.1063/1.3324702) applied MTA to π-conjugated systems. For 1D conjugated molecules the MTA energy error is <1 mEh. For "small/medium-sized two-dimensional π-conjugated molecules" energies are off "by a few millihartrees", but gradients match well enough for MTA geometry optimisation. **No Hessians.** Europe PMC lists 42 citing papers; none applies MTA Hessians to 2D π systems. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.3324702%22); [citations MED/20210384](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/20210384/citations?format=json)
- Chan & Karton (Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 23, 17713–17723, 2021, DOI 10.1039/d1cp01659h) found that for medium-sized aromatic hydrocarbons, a good way to set up a balanced isodesmic reaction is "to split the target species into two halves with an aromatic overlapping region". This is thermochemistry only. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1039%2Fd1cp01659h%22)
- Crisci, Lazzari, Uribe & Barone (J. Chem. Phys. 2026, 064104, DOI 10.1063/5.0343929), "Topology-dependent refinement of molecular structures and vibrational properties": a transferable, topology-dependent local correction to a double-hybrid reference. Its bond-field level-1 term "contains the conjugation term", and "selected vibrational residuals are represented in normal-mode or fragment space". It is assessed on PAHs, OH⋯O systems, carbohydrates and a nucleoside; rotational-constant errors near 1% drop by more than an order of magnitude. The abstract gives no frequency numbers or PAH sizes. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0343929%22)
- Barone, Crisci & Mendolicchio (J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2026, 7326–7343, DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.6c00816), "Multilevel Fragmentation and Boundary Corrections for Accurate Vibrational Spectra of Large Molecules", targets VPT2 for 30–50-atom molecules. A high-level correction for a dominant fragment is carried through the anharmonic treatment; for two equal fragments the harmonic correction is "assembled at the frequency level through a mode-resolved combination", which "avoids the projection and Hessian reconstruction steps that often complicate fragment-based anharmonic treatments". A local harmonic correction for "electronically delicate boundary bonds", in internal coordinates and transferable from compact auxiliary models, addresses the residual link-atom error. Tests: benzonitrile (nitrile at high level, aromatic ring at low level), tryptophan, uridine. No PAH. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.6c00816%22)
- Hanson-Heine, George & Besley (J. Chem. Phys. 136, 224102, 2012, DOI 10.1063/1.4727853) combined partial-Hessian normal modes with VPT2/TOSH for anharmonic frequencies of localised modes, including "the C-H stretching region of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons". The results are "in close agreement" with full anharmonic calculations at much lower cost. This is a partial Hessian of the full molecule, not assembly from fragments. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.4727853%22)
- Herbert's 2019 Perspective lists a fragment-based MP2/cc-pVTZ **energy** of a (C150H30)2 nanographene dimer "in under an hour on 71 288 processors". This is an energy, not a Hessian. — [Herbert, J. Chem. Phys. 151, 170901 (2019), DOI 10.1063/1.5126216, PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- Searches (web and Europe PMC) for fragment/MTA/MIM/GEBF/FMO/SMF combined with Hessian/frequencies and PAH/graphene/nanographene/conjugated found nothing beyond the items above. The web search summaries said the same: no fragment-Hessian PAH paper. — [web search summary 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22713031); [Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=fragment%20Hessian%20polycyclic%20aromatic)

### Inferences
- A defensible rewording of the claim: "Fragment-assembled Hessians and IR spectra are established for peptides, proteins, carbohydrates and clusters. Aromatic-sextet-preserving PAH fragmentation (AroBOROS, AROFRAG) is established for energies. To our knowledge, no one has assembled a fused PAH's Hessian or vibrational correction from overlapping PAH fragments and benchmarked it against the full calculation." The unqualified claim "never been tried" is too strong, because the generic method (overlapping-fragment Hessians and fragment-based Δ-corrections) is 15+ years old.
- AroBOROS's fragments (naphthalene C10H8 = 18 atoms; biphenyl C12H10 = 22 atoms) fall exactly in the 10–30-atom range named in the claim. A reviewer will likely ask "why not AroBOROS-MIM Hessians?". The natural baseline would be AroBOROS/AROFRAG fragmentation extended to second derivatives, which MIM already supports through Jacobian link-atom projection (Jose & Raghavachari 2015).
- The Barone group (Pisa) is the most active competitor in 2026 on transferable or fragment-based vibrational corrections that include PAHs. Their full texts (JCP 064104 and JCTC 7326) should be read before claiming novelty.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full text of Crisci et al. 2026 (JCP 064104). I do not know which PAHs, what sizes, or whether its "fragment space" vibrational residuals are applied to large PAH frequencies.
- Europe PMC forward-citation lists are incomplete (they cover PMC/MEDLINE-indexed citing works). Google Scholar citation trees were not checked.
- ChemRxiv/arXiv preprints from 2025–2026 were only searched through general web search; a dedicated ChemRxiv search was not possible.

## Molecules-in-Molecules (MIM; Raghavachari, Mayhall, Jose, Thapa, Beckett) for Hessians, IR, Raman, VCD, ROA

### Takeaway
MIM Hessians (Jacobian link-atom projection, ONIOM-like) are published for IR (2015), Raman (2015), VCD (2015) and ROA (2016). Benchmarks are peptides (25 molecules) and carbohydrates (10 enantiomer pairs). Applications include polystyrene (pendant phenyls), cryptophane-A (non-fused benzene rings), perhydrotriphenylene (saturated), α-cyclodextrin and d-maltose. No PAH or graphene Hessian was found. The MIM PAH fragmentation (AroBOROS) stayed at energies.

### Cited Findings
- MIM was introduced by Mayhall & Raghavachari (J. Chem. Theory Comput. 7, 1336, 2011, DOI 10.1021/ct200033b) as an ONIOM-like multilayer extrapolated fragment method. — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ct200033b)
- Jose & Raghavachari (J. Chem. Theory Comput. 11, 950–961, 2015, DOI 10.1021/ct501026m): overlapping subsystems from a number-based scheme, link-H capping, and Jacobian projection of link-atom gradient, force-constant and dipole-derivative components onto host atoms. Benchmark: 25 small-to-large peptides. Adding a low-level long-range layer (MIM2) improves energies by 80% and "IR frequencies and intensities have an ∼75% improvement" over MIM1. The same holds for anion, cation and radical variants. The abstract gives no absolute cm⁻¹ values. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fct501026m%22)
- Herbert (2019) summarises MIM2 vibrational spectroscopy: high level typically triple-ζ DFT on fragments plus a low-level (double-ζ DFT or semiempirical) supersystem; "Vibrational frequencies are generally accurate to within a few cm−1 as compared to supersystem benchmarks", and the low-level supersystem "helps to correct relative intensities". Applications: Raman of polystyrene (B3LYP/6-311+G** high, HF/6-31G low) and VCD of perhydrotriphenylene (MPW1PW91/aug-cc-pVTZ-f high, MPW1PW91/6-31+G* low). — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- Raman: Jose & Raghavachari, Mol. Phys. 113, 3057–3066 (2015), "Molecules-in-molecules fragment-based method for the evaluation of Raman spectra of large molecules" (cited as ref. 365 in Herbert 2019). — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- VCD: Jose, Beckett & Raghavachari, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 11, 4238–4247 (2015), DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.5b00647. Calibrated on d/l enantiomers of 10 carbohydrates, with frequencies and VCD intensities "accurately reproduced". Applied to perhydrotriphenylene and cryptophane-A. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.5b00647%22)
- ROA: Jose & Raghavachari, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 12, 585–594 (2016), DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.5b01127. 10 carbohydrate pairs, then d-maltose, α-D-cyclodextrin and cryptophane-A. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.5b01127%22)
- VCD/ROA of alanine polypeptides: Jose & Raghavachari, Chirality 2016, DOI 10.1002/chir.22651. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/chir.22651)
- Europe PMC lists 24 citing papers for MIM-IR 2015. None applies MIM Hessians to PAHs (they are proteins, NMR, protein–ligand, fragmentation comparisons). — [citations MED/26579749](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/26579749/citations?format=json)

### Inferences
- The MIM machinery for Hessians plus the AroBOROS PAH fragment generator (same group) together make the obvious prior-art route to the claimed idea. Its absence from the literature is the strongest support for the narrowed claim.

### Gaps
- Absolute MIM frequency errors (cm⁻¹, mean/max) and fragment sizes for the 25-peptide benchmark were not retrievable (ACS paywall/403).

## Generalized energy-based fragmentation (GEBF; Li Shuhua, Li Wei, Hua Weijie)

### Takeaway
GEBF Hessians and IR spectra exist since 2008 (embedded-charge subsystems, linear scaling). Applications: polypeptide and water cluster (2008), molecular crystals (2016, 2023, up to 864 atoms per cell), ionic-liquid clusters (2018). I found no conjugated or PAH vibrational application. An instructive pitfall: fragments that carry non-integer charge give large errors.

### Cited Findings
- Hua, Fang, Li, Yu & Li, J. Phys. Chem. A 112, 10864–10872 (2008), DOI 10.1021/jp8026385: each subsystem is embedded in point charges of all other atoms. Tests on "a polypeptide and a water cluster" give "quite reliable" geometries, frequencies and thermochemistry and "satisfactory descriptions for vibrational intensities, for general molecules with polar or charged groups". The abstract gives no cm⁻¹ values. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fjp8026385%22)
- Fang, Jia & Li, J. Phys. Chem. A 120, 2700–2711 (2016), DOI 10.1021/acs.jpca.5b10927: PBC-GEBF vibrational spectra of CO2, imidazole, urea and ammonia-borane crystals (MP2). — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jpca.5b10927%22)
- Hong, Fang, Li & Li, J. Chem. Phys. 158, 044117 (2023), DOI 10.1063/5.0137072: PBC-GEBF IR of a molecular crystal with 864 atoms per unit cell (DFT). — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0137072%22)
- Li, Yuan, Wang, Li & Li, Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 20, 13547 (2018), DOI 10.1039/c8cp00513c: ion-based fragmentation fails because "the non-integer charge behavior of each ion ... may induce significant errors" when every fragment is assumed to carry an integer charge; ion-pair fragmentation fixes it. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1039%2Fc8cp00513c%22)
- GEBF for localized excited states (Li, Li, Lin & Li, J. Phys. Chem. A 2016, DOI 10.1021/acs.jpca.6b11193) and emission spectra (Du et al., J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2022) exist. The 2016 tests use π→π* transitions of pyridine and uracil in solution, i.e. small chromophores. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jpca.6b11193%22); [citations of GEBF 2008](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/18837491/citations?format=json)
- The 83 Europe PMC citing papers of GEBF-2008 include proteins, water, ionic liquids, crystals, VCD of (S)-alternarlactam in the solid state (Jiang, Tan & Ma, J. Phys. Chem. B 2011) and the DCMB method. None is a PAH or graphene Hessian. — [citations MED/18837491](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/18837491/citations?format=json)

### Inferences
- The ionic-liquid finding carries over to PAH **cations**: if a cation's positive charge is delocalised over the whole π framework, a capped fragment cannot carry the right fractional charge. This is a warning for fragment Hessians of PAH cations (relevant to the project's cation rows).

### Gaps
- GEBF frequency errors in cm⁻¹ and fragment sizes for the 2008 polypeptide test are not in the abstract.

## FMO Hessians (Fedorov, Kitaura, Nakata, Nagata, Komeiji)

### Takeaway
Analytic FMO Hessians exist for RHF (2013), UHF (2014), DFT (2015), DFTB (2016) and with electrostatic embedding (2018). They have been used for IR/Raman of polypeptides, water clusters and the proteins Trp-cage and crambin. The accuracy benchmark is 3 cm⁻¹ RMS on polyalanine with two residues per fragment. I found no conjugated/PAH application. Herbert notes spurious delocalisation of fragment wavefunctions in large basis sets.

### Cited Findings
- Nakata, Nagata, Fedorov, Yokojima, Kitaura & Nakamura, J. Chem. Phys. 138, 164103 (2013), DOI 10.1063/1.4800990: analytic FMO Hessian with approximations in the CPHF equations (GAMESS). With "two residues per fragment" the "accuracy of 3 cm(-1) in the reduced mean square deviation of vibrational frequencies from ab initio for all three polyalanine isomers", ZPE error ≤0.3 kcal/mol. Also tested on a water cluster. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.4800990%22)
- Follow-ups: Raman spectra with FMO (Nakata et al., J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2014); FMO3-DFT analytic Hessian (Nakata et al., J. Chem. Phys. 2015); FMO-DFTB analytic Hessian (Nakata, Nishimoto & Fedorov, J. Chem. Phys. 2016); IR and Raman in solution (Nakata & Fedorov, Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2019); electrostatic-embedding Hessian (Nakata & Fedorov, J. Comput. Chem. 2018). — [citations of Rahalkar 2008, Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/19102520/citations?format=json)
- The 2018 embedding paper simulated IR spectra of Trp-cage (1L2Y) and crambin (1CRN). — [catalogue record](https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/NLM289347858/Description)
- Cost: "the FMO2 Hessian for a molecule with 400 atoms can be evaluated in 16 h on 72 cores at the B3LYP/6-31G* level of theory, with a total memory requirement of 1.6 Gb". FMO+PCM analytic and numerical Hessians give slightly different frequencies. — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- Herbert: when Pauli repulsion between fragments is absent, "as in the FMO approach, then problems arise with spurious delocalization of the fragment wave functions in large basis sets". — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)

### Inferences
- FMO fragments are normally separated at single (sp3) bonds. A fused aromatic framework has no single bonds to cut, so standard FMO fragmentation does not apply naturally to PAHs (inference; see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I did not find an FMO paper on how bond detachment handles conjugated or aromatic bonds, nor any FMO Hessian of a conjugated system. The statement that standard FMO detaches at sp3 bonds is background knowledge, not verified in this session.

## Molecular tailoring approach (MTA; Gadre, Ganesh, Rahalkar, Sahu, Khire)

### Takeaway
MTA Hessians date from 2008. Grafted MTA (2015) and Fragments-in-Fragments (2016) reach ≤3 cm⁻¹ (max) against full calculations on polypeptides. MTA IR for proteins up to 407 atoms gives errors of about 5 cm⁻¹ (2023). MTASpec software exists (2021), and VCD (2025) and ROA (2025) followed. Gadre's group is the only one with a dedicated π-conjugated fragmentation study (2010, energies and gradients, no Hessians). The 2025 MTA-VCD paper mentions oxo-helicene, an ortho-fused aromatic; the abstract does not say whether MTA was used for it.

### Cited Findings
- Rahalkar, Ganesh & Gadre, J. Chem. Phys. 129, 234101 (2008), DOI 10.1063/1.2978387: cardinality-guided MTA Hessian and frequencies for "spatially extended molecules" (HF, DFT, a few MP2), with "substantial gains with negligible loss of accuracy". It is also applied to larger molecules whose Hessians do not fit on PC hardware. The abstract gives no cm⁻¹ values or molecule list. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.2978387%22)
- Water clusters: in the MTA MP2 spectrum of a water cluster, 11 primary fragments ((H2O)6–(H2O)8) plus 22 intersections give 33 subsystem Hessians, "none larger than (H2O)8". The result agrees with the full Hessian "except for an error in the relative intensities of a pair of peaks around 3300 cm−1", which disappears after grafting (Sahu & Gadre, J. Chem. Phys. 142, 014107, 2015). — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- Grafted MTA (Herbert's description): fragments of "≈100 atoms in some cases" plus a full-system low-level Hessian (e.g. BP86/6-31G or MP2/6-31G) give "essentially exact results" at the higher level (BP86/TZVP or MP2/6-31G**), with the high level applied only to fragments. Caveat: MTA was not coupled to geometry optimisation at the same level, so "the geometry is not quite stationary at the level of theory used to compute frequencies". — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- Fragments-in-Fragments (Sahu & Gadre, J. Chem. Phys. 144, 114113, 2016, DOI 10.1063/1.4943966): grafting without any full calculation, relying on "the nearly basis-set-independent nature of the MTA-based error". Systems: β-strand, 3₁₀- and α-helix of Ac(Ala)nNH2 (n = 10, 15), gramicidin S conformers, trpzip2 (1LE1), at BP86/TZVP, M06/6-311G**, M05-2X/6-31G**. "For most of the cases, a maximum difference of 3 cm(-1)" from the full calculation. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.4943966%22)
- Proteins (Sahu, Khire & Gadre, J. Chem. Phys. 158, 044309, 2023, DOI 10.1063/5.0149572): CAM-B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ geometry optimisation and IR of ten proteins up to 407 atoms and 6617 basis functions. Typical errors against full calculations: 5–10 mEh in energy and 5 cm⁻¹ in IR frequencies. The time, memory and disk advantage grows with size. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0149572%22)
- MTASpec software (Comput. Phys. Commun. 2021) automates fragmentation, low-basis MTA plus full calculation, and Hessian synthesis for IR/Raman. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010465521002873)
- MTA-VCD (Sharma, Khire, Gadre & Sahu, J. Chem. Phys. 162, 244103, 2025, DOI 10.1063/5.0271469): grafting correction from a smaller basis. Tested on sugars, polypeptides and proteins; "a comparison of gas- and solvent-phase VCD spectra of Oxo-helicene shows excellent agreement with the experimental spectrum". — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0271469%22)
- MTA-ROA: Sharma, Khire & Sahu, J. Comput. Chem. 2025, e70276, DOI 10.1002/jcc.70276. — [Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22molecular%20tailoring%22%20AND%20Raman)
- π-conjugated MTA (Yeole & Gadre 2010): 1D <1 mEh; 2D "a few millihartrees", gradients good enough for optimisation. No Hessians (see the claim section). — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.3324702%22)
- The IAS review "Tailoring approach for exploring vibrational features of large molecular systems" (Khire, Sahu & Gadre, J. Chem. Sci. 137, 2025) is the most recent overview; full text returned 403. — [IAS](https://www.ias.ac.in/article/fulltext/jcsc/137/0072)

### Inferences
- MTA grafting, and MIM2, are exactly a "vibrational Δ-correction assembled from pieces": a high-minus-low Hessian difference evaluated on fragments and added to a cheap full-system Hessian. So the claim must not say this kind of correction is new. Only its application to fused PAHs is unreported.
- Oxo-helicene is the only fused-aromatic system I found in an MTA vibrational paper. Whether its spectrum was computed with MTA (and with what fragments and error) needs a full-text check before the claim is published.

### Gaps
- No access to the Rahalkar 2008 test-molecule table, the MTASpec benchmark table, or the oxo-helicene details.

## MFCC, many-body expansion (Herbert, Richard), systematic fragmentation (Collins), PHVA, mobile-block Hessian, Cartesian tensor transfer

### Takeaway
All of these families have vibrational versions. SMFA Hessians (Collins 2014) give mean errors of 0.7–2.4 cm⁻¹ and maxima of 6–17 cm⁻¹ on proteins, (Gly)12 and water. SMFA by construction never cuts multiple bonds. Cartesian tensor transfer (Bouř, since 1997) builds whole-molecule Hessians and intensity tensors from fragments of up to ~100 atoms, for peptides and nucleic acids. PHVA and MBH need full-system electronic structure and constrain parts of the system; they are not fragment assembly. None was applied to fused PAHs.

### Cited Findings
- SMFA (Collins, J. Chem. Phys. 141, 094108, 2014, DOI 10.1063/1.4894185), HF/6-31G(d), Table II (whole-molecule vs fragmented optimisation and frequencies). Mean / max absolute frequency errors: GelA Level 3 0.8 / 6.3 cm⁻¹ (max fragment basis 216 of 710 functions); GelB L3 0.7 / 10.5; (H2O)28 L2 5.7 / 62.2, L3 2.5 / 17.1, L2 with charges 1.6 / 17.3, L3 with charges 0.7 / 8.0 (209/532 functions); (Gly)12 L3 with charges 2.4 / 8.8 (140/881). The test set "contain[s] aromatic rings" as side chains (His, Phe). — [ANU open repository PDF](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/16184/1/01_Collins_Molecular_forces%2c_geometries%2c_2014.pdf)
- SMFA group rule (Appendix A): a bond is "multiple" if shorter than the sum of covalent radii + 0.08 Å and an atom lacks its normal valence, and "Two atoms connected by a multiple bond are contained in the same group". — [ANU PDF](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/16184/1/01_Collins_Molecular_forces%2c_geometries%2c_2014.pdf)
- Herbert (2019): vibrational modes "tend to localize within individual chemical moieties or functional groups", with exceptions such as amide I coupling, where "the excitation [delocalizes] over several residues". These effects "emerge naturally from diagonalization of a Hessian that is constructed from local information". — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- N-body:many-body QM:QM vibrational frequencies for small H-bonded clusters: Howard & Tschumper, J. Chem. Phys. 2013. — [citations list, Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/19102520/citations?format=json)
- MFCC-type protein Hessians: Liu, Zhang & He, "Fragment quantum chemical approach to geometry optimization and vibrational spectrum calculation of proteins", Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2016. — [citations of GEBF-2008, Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/18837491/citations?format=json)
- Fragme∩t (Broderick, …, Herbert, WIREs Comput. Mol. Sci. 2025, DOI 10.1002/wcms.70058), an open-source fragmentation framework. A search summary reports finite-difference frequencies with mean errors <1 cm⁻¹ and maximum errors <10 cm⁻¹ against analytic results (B3LYP/aug-cc-pVTZ); I did not verify whether that comparison was fragmented or not. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12700225/)
- Delocalisation error "poisons the density-functional many-body expansion" (Broderick & Herbert, Chem. Sci. 2024); energy-based screening can forestall divergence. — [RSC open access](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/sc/d4sc05955g)
- DCMB (Wu & Xu, J. Comput. Chem. 33, 1421, 2012, DOI 10.1002/jcc.22973): divide-and-conquer plus mixed basis, with all calculations on the whole molecule and "no artificial caps", "particularly useful for charged and/or delocalized systems". It gives geometries, energies and frequencies. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1002%2Fjcc.22973%22)
- Mobile-block Hessian: Ghysels, Van Neck & Waroquier, J. Chem. Phys. 127, 164108 (2007); adjoined blocks, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 5, 1203 (2009); QM/MM MBH, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 7, 496 (2011). Intensities in MBH: Terrett et al., Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 19, 6654 (2017), which promises "potentially dramatically reducing computational expense" for regions of interest. — [Europe PMC MBH search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22mobile%20block%20Hessian%22)
- PHVA anharmonic for PAH C–H stretches: Hanson-Heine et al. 2012 (see the claim section). — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.4727853%22)
- Cartesian coordinate tensor transfer (CCT; Bouř et al., since 1997): atomic tensors (Hessian, APT, AAT, polarisability derivatives) from small fragments are transferred in Cartesian coordinates, enabling spectra "for large biomolecular systems, currently with up to several thousands of atoms" (Andrushchenko & Bouř, Chirality 22, E96, 2010, DOI 10.1002/chir.20872: DNA and RNA). — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1002%2Fchir.20872%22)
- Yamamoto, Li, Ruud & Bouř, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 8, 977 (2012), DOI 10.1021/ct200714h: transfer accuracy "varies according to the molecule and the tensor type". VCD and ROA are "significantly more sensitive" than IR and Raman. Helices with intramolecular H-bonds "required larger fragments for the same accuracy". Overall there are substantial savings of computer time. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fct200714h%22)
- Bouř-group chapter: fragments "with up to ∼100 atoms" give a reasonable balance of accuracy and cost. — [Springer chapter, 10.1007/978-981-10-5651-2_8](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/978-981-10-5651-2_8)
- A related paper notes that a CCT-type assembled Hessian "might yield several imaginary frequencies when diagonalizing the CCT Hessian". — [search result, Collins/related](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/16184/1/01_Collins_Molecular_forces%2c_geometries%2c_2014.pdf) (attribution uncertain; it came from a search snippet)

### Inferences
- Under SMFA's rule (iii), every C–C bond in a PAH is short and the carbons are trivalent, so all bonds count as "multiple". The whole fused carbon skeleton then becomes one group, and SMFA cannot fragment a PAH at all (inference from the stated rule, not a statement by Collins).
- CCT is methodologically the closest to "assemble a big Hessian from pieces and transfer it". In principle it is method-agnostic, but its only demonstrated uses are biopolymers made of repeating units joined by single bonds.

### Gaps
- No cm⁻¹ benchmark for CCT band positions was found.
- Cardinality-guided MBE (Richard & Herbert) Hessian papers with numbers were not retrieved.

## Transferable force constants and force-field transfer between PAHs

### Takeaway
Transfer of scaling factors and force fields from small to large PAHs is old: a 1992 MNDO-SQM study gave mean errors of 25–30 cm⁻¹. Modern large-PAH anharmonic work instead uses full calculations at cheaper levels (reparameterised PM6 quartic force fields up to C30H16 in 2025; Pisa composite schemes for corannulene and sumanene in 2026), or exploits trends with size (Mackie et al.). I found no modern paper that transfers Cartesian or internal force constants from small PAHs to build a large PAH Hessian and benchmarks it.

### Cited Findings
- "Vibrational modelling in large polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons" (J. Mol. Struct. THEOCHEM 1992, PII 0166-1280(92)80085-Z): MNDO SQM force fields for a PAH series, with scale factors per coordinate type fitted on benzene and naphthalene (and their deuterated forms) and transferred to larger PAHs. Redundant transferred coordinates needed a modified non-redundant, symmetry-preserving scaling. Using naphthalene-derived instead of benzene-derived factors cut the mean frequency error from 30 to 25 cm⁻¹ (numbers from a search summary; the page itself returned 403). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/016612809280085Z)
- Mackie, Candian, Lee & Tielens, Theor. Chem. Acc. 140, 124 (2021), DOI 10.1007/s00214-021-02807-z: "the absolute value of the large negative individual anharmonic constants decreases with size of the PAH, however, since the number of vibrational modes increases, the total sum of the anharmonic constants remains nearly constant". This size trend could support transferring corrections. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8549957)
- QFF cost: converting quartic force constants from normal to Cartesian coordinates involves (3N)⁴ values for large molecules; quartic calculations for pyrene or coronene need 36N² Hessians. — [Mackie thesis, Leiden](https://local.strw.leidenuniv.nl/events/phdtheses/mackie/Thesis.pdf); [Publisher's note, J. Chem. Phys. 149, 144102](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328925772_Publisher's_Note_Anharmonic_vibrational_spectroscopy_of_polycyclic_aromatic_hydrocarbons_PAHs_J_Chem_Phys_149_144102_2018)
- Sehring, Johns, Esposito & Fortenberry, J. Phys. Chem. A 129, 6623 (2025), DOI 10.1021/acs.jpca.5c03259: reparameterised PM6 reproduces experimental PAH IR "with nearly the same accuracy as state-of-the-art quantum chemical methods", with full explicit QFFs. First anharmonic spectra of benzo[a]pyrene (C20H14), naphtho[2,1,8-qra]naphthacene (C24H14) and naphtho[2,3-b]anthanthrene (C30H16); "orders of magnitude faster". — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jpca.5c03259%22)
- Barone, Crisci, Di Grande, Lazzari, Mendolicchio & Uribe, J. Chem. Phys. 2026, 114309, DOI 10.1063/5.0313437: Pisa composite schemes plus GVPT2 with mechanical and electrical anharmonicity for indene, azulene, corannulene and sumanene. Fundamentals with "typical deviations of 5-10 cm-1". These are full-molecule calculations, not fragment-based. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0313437%22)
- Barone, Lazzari & Mendolicchio, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2026, 2453–2466, DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.5c02123: primary/auxiliary/spectator mode partitioning (perturb-then-diagonalize). External modes are handled with finite differences of analytic gradients instead of Hessians; dual-level harmonic/anharmonic. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.5c02123%22)
- A force-field review notes that the valence functional form "is not flexible enough for fused-ring systems such as naphthalene"; a common graphite/PAH force field (Mapelli et al. 1999) was only seen as a citation. — [search summary; PMC11171666](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11171666/)

### Inferences
- The project's "vibrational correction from pieces" differs from scale-factor transfer (a single number per coordinate type) and from size trends (Mackie). It transfers a *learned or computed block correction* from overlapping fragments. The 1992 result (25–30 cm⁻¹ with transferred SQM factors) is a useful historical baseline showing why naive transfer is not enough.

### Gaps
- Cyvin-type transferable valence force fields for PAHs (1980s), Pulay SQM applications to PAHs (e.g. Langhoff, Bauschlicher, Martin), and the Mapelli et al. 1999 graphite/PAH force field were not retrieved with numbers.
- I found no Mackie/Candian/Tielens paper that explicitly transfers anharmonic constants from small to large PAHs; the 11.2 µm paper only reports the size trend.

## Pitfalls authors report for conjugated and aromatic systems

### Takeaway
The pitfalls documented in the literature are: (1) energy errors several times larger for 2D than for 1D conjugated systems (Yeole & Gadre 2010); (2) the need to keep aromatic sextets intact and follow Clar structures (AroBOROS, AROFRAG); (3) cutting rules that forbid breaking multiple bonds (SMFA); (4) residual link-atom error at "electronically delicate" boundary bonds (Barone 2026); (5) fragments that cannot carry fractional or delocalised charge (GEBF ionic liquids); (6) spurious delocalisation of fragment wavefunctions in large basis sets, and delocalisation error in DFT-based MBE (Herbert); (7) intensities and polarised spectra being more sensitive than frequencies (Bouř; MIM1 vs MIM2; MTA water). I found no source that quantifies fragment-Hessian errors for delocalised low-frequency collective modes (drumhead, out-of-plane) of large PAHs.

### Cited Findings
- 2D π-conjugated MTA energies are off "by a few millihartrees" against <1 mEh for 1D; gradients stay usable. — [Yeole & Gadre 2010, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.3324702%22)
- AroBOROS: errors fall below chemical accuracy only "by combining subsystems that reflect the lowest energy structures determined by Clar's rule of aromatic sextets". — [Noffke et al. 2020, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.9b00566%22)
- AROFRAG treats the sextet ring as "a single unbreakable unit so that the basic unit of aromaticity remains preserved". — [Masoumifeshani & Korona 2024, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.3c00875%22)
- SMFA: atoms joined by a multiple bond are always in the same group. — [Collins 2014 PDF](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/16184/1/01_Collins_Molecular_forces%2c_geometries%2c_2014.pdf)
- "the standard link atom approximation may leave a residual error concentrated in the harmonic force field" at electronically delicate boundary bonds; fixed with a transferable local internal-coordinate correction. — [Barone, Crisci & Mendolicchio 2026, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.6c00816%22)
- Non-integer fragment charges cause significant GEBF errors. — [Li et al. 2018, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1039%2Fc8cp00513c%22)
- Spurious delocalisation of fragment wavefunctions in large basis sets (FMO-type, no Pauli repulsion). — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)
- DCMB was motivated by the fact that capping is problematic "for charged and/or delocalized systems". — [Wu & Xu 2012, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1002%2Fjcc.22973%22)
- Intensities: MIM2's low-level supersystem layer gives about 75% better frequencies and intensities than MIM1 and corrects relative intensities. MTA's water-cluster relative-intensity error near 3300 cm⁻¹ needed grafting. VCD/ROA transfer is more sensitive than IR/Raman. — [Jose & Raghavachari 2015](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fct501026m%22); [Herbert 2019](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf); [Yamamoto et al. 2012](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fct200714h%22)
- Modes localise "within individual chemical moieties", with the exception of coupled oscillators (amide I). — [Herbert 2019 PDF](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)

### Inferences
- In a PAH, the "chemical moiety" is the whole π framework, so Herbert's locality argument (which explains why fragment Hessians work for peptides) is weakest there. In-plane C–H stretches and C–H out-of-plane modes are local (solo/duo/trio/quartet hydrogens), which explains why partial-Hessian treatment of PAH C–H stretches works (Hanson-Heine 2012). Skeletal and low-frequency out-of-plane modes are delocalised, so this is where fragment errors should be largest. This is an inference that fits the project's own finding that out-of-plane and low modes are the hard family.
- Fragment sizes that work for peptides (2 residues ≈ 20 atoms for FMO; ~100 atoms for CCT/grafted MTA) cannot be carried over to PAHs without testing. The Bouř result that H-bonded helices need larger fragments suggests that strongly coupled frameworks need larger overlap.
- A fragment scheme for PAH Hessians should, according to the energy-fragmentation literature, use aromatic, Clar-consistent subsystems (AroBOROS/AROFRAG-type) rather than link-H caps on broken aromatic bonds.

### Gaps
- No source found on fragment-Hessian errors for drumhead or low-frequency out-of-plane modes of large PAHs, or on long-range force-constant decay in PAH Hessians (how fast Hessian blocks fall off with bond distance in a π system).
- No source found on fragment Hessians of PAH radical cations, or on open-shell fragment Hessians of conjugated systems.
