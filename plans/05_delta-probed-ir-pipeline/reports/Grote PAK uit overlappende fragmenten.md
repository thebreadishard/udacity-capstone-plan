# Fragment Hessians abound, but skip fused PAHs

The claim as worded, that building a large PAH's vibrational correction from overlapping corpus-size pieces "has never been tried", is too strong. A narrower version survives a targeted search. The construction itself is fifteen years old: a cheap full-molecule Hessian plus high-minus-low corrections computed on overlapping fragments, mapped back through link-atom Jacobians. MIM2 (2015), grafted MTA (2015) and N-body:many-body CCSD(T):MP2 (2013) all publish it for frequencies, within a few tenths to a few cm⁻¹ of the unfragmented calculation. Plain fragment Hessians at one level (MTA 2008, GEBF 2008, FMO 2013, SMFA 2014, and Bouř's tensor transfer since 1997) are just as established; where errors are reported, they run from 0.6 to 5 cm⁻¹ mean. Every one of these benchmarks is on peptides, proteins, carbohydrates, water clusters or molecular crystals, not on fused PAHs. The PAH-specific fragmentation schemes (AroBOROS 2020, AROFRAG 2024) cut along Clar sextets into naphthalene- and biphenyl-sized pieces of 18–22 atoms, which is exactly our piece size, but they stop at energies. The Lego-brick and templating-synthon work stops at geometries. Nobody we found has assembled a 34–60-atom fused PAH's Hessian, Δ-Hessian or per-mode correction from overlapping PAH pieces and benchmarked it against the full molecule. The one real threat is the Barone group's 2026 work. One paper represents vibrational residuals "in normal-mode or fragment space" and was tested on PAHs. A second assembles fragment harmonic corrections mode by mode for VPT2. Both are closed access and were read as abstracts only. Until their full texts are read, the claim may be stated only as "not found in a targeted search", scoped to fused PAHs, to a high-minus-low correction, and to pieces that are reusable corpus molecules.

Four of the open-access papers were afterwards read in full (11 Nov 2026): Laurens 2021, Tang 2026, Collins 2014 and Burger 2026 (notes `research_notes/Grote PAK uit overlappende fragmenten/fulltext_*.md`). None of them assembles a Hessian, a correction or a spectrum from pieces, so the narrow claim stands; their numbers and wording are corrected below. The other key papers, the closed Barone ones above all, are still read as abstracts only.

## The fragment Δ-Hessian is a solved construction outside aromatics

The algebra our idea relies on is published and has names. ONIOM's two-layer extrapolation, E(high, real) ≈ E(low, real) + [E(high, model) − E(low, model)], gives Hessians by differentiating the expression, and Gaussian prints ONIOM frequencies per layer. NWChem's documentation adds the condition that matters for us: it works when the model region contains the interactions that dominate the difference ([NWChem ONIOM doc](https://grok.dragonflybsd.org/xref/dports/science/nwchem-data/nwchem-7.0.2-release/doc/user/oniom.tex?a=true)). Molecules-in-Molecules (MIM) generalises this to many overlapping, link-H-capped subsystems. Jose and Raghavachari project each link atom's force-constant and dipole-derivative contributions back onto its host atoms with a Jacobian. On 25 peptides, adding a low-level full-molecule layer (MIM2) improved **IR frequencies and intensities by about 75% over the one-layer MIM1** ([Jose & Raghavachari 2015, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fct501026m%22)). The abstract gives no absolute cm⁻¹ numbers. Herbert's review gives the general verdict: MIM2 frequencies are "generally accurate to within a few cm⁻¹" of supersystem benchmarks, and the low-level supersystem corrects relative intensities ([Herbert 2019](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)). MIM was extended to VCD on ten carbohydrate enantiomer pairs and to ROA ([Jose, Beckett & Raghavachari 2015](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.5b00647%22); [Jose & Raghavachari 2016](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.5b01127%22)). Its applications include polystyrene and cryptophane-A, which have benzene rings, but none of them is fused.

The Gadre group's molecular tailoring approach (MTA) has the longest Hessian record and the most direct analogue of our scheme. Cardinality-guided MTA Hessians date from 2008 ([Rahalkar, Ganesh & Gadre 2008](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.2978387%22)). "Grafted" MTA adds a fragment-assembled higher-minus-lower correction to a full lower-level calculation. For (H₂O)₁₆ at MP2 it lands **within 2 cm⁻¹ of the full MP2 frequencies** (a figure from a search extract of the abstract) ([peeref record](https://peeref.com/zh/works/1090207)). Fragments-in-Fragments drops the full calculation altogether and gives "a maximum difference of 3 cm⁻¹" on Ac(Ala)ₙNH₂ helices and strands (n = 10, 15), gramicidin S and trpzip2 ([Sahu & Gadre 2016](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.4943966%22)). At CAM-B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ, ten proteins of up to **407 atoms** come out at about **5 cm⁻¹** against the full calculation ([Sahu, Khire & Gadre 2023](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0149572%22)). Herbert records one caveat that carries over to any transfer scheme. MTA was not coupled to geometry optimisation at the same level, so "the geometry is not quite stationary" at the level used for the frequencies ([Herbert 2019](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)).

The correlated-level version also exists, though only for weakly bound clusters. Howard and Tschumper computed the 1- to N-body terms at CCSD(T) and everything above at MP2, which amounts to a full MP2 Hessian plus a fragment CCSD(T)−MP2 correction. On (H₂O)ₙ with n = 3–7, the 2-body version stays within a few cm⁻¹ of canonical CCSD(T), and the 3-body version within **a few tenths of a cm⁻¹** ([BVB abstract record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_1467065261); [Tschumper, Sanibel 2013](https://sanibelsymposium.qtp.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/20//2013/Tschumper,%20Gregory.pdf)). That convergence comes from monomers that hardly couple. A PAH has no monomers, and every cut goes through a conjugated C–C bond, so this evidence does not transfer.

The single-level fragment families fill out the picture. Systematic Molecular Fragmentation (SMFA), at HF/6-31G(d), reaches **mean/max frequency errors of 0.7/10.5 to 2.4/8.8 cm⁻¹** on two charged, mostly aliphatic molecules (GelA 0.8/6.3, GelB 0.7/10.5; labels from Hua et al. 2008) and (Gly)₁₂, and 0.7/8.0 cm⁻¹ on (H₂O)₂₈ with embedded charges; at MP2/6-31+G(d,p) on zwitterion chains NH₃⁺(CH₂)ₙCOO⁻ the mean is 0.9–1.4 and the max grows with length from 4.2 to 9.4 cm⁻¹ (Table I). SMFA uses one level throughout, with no low-level full-molecule layer, and its bond rules keep atoms joined by a multiple bond in one group, so a fused PAH would stay a single group; no aromatic or conjugated system is tested (full text, App. A; [Collins 2014, ANU open PDF](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/16184/1/01_Collins_Molecular_forces%2c_geometries%2c_2014.pdf)). Analytic FMO Hessians with two residues per fragment give **3 cm⁻¹** reduced mean-square deviation on polyalanine ([Nakata et al. 2013](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.4800990%22)). GEBF Hessians with embedded charges have run on polypeptides, water, ionic liquids and crystals of up to 864 atoms per cell ([Hua et al. 2008](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fjp8026385%22); [Hong et al. 2023](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0137072%22)). Bouř's Cartesian coordinate tensor transfer (CCT) is methodologically the closest to "assemble a large Hessian from transferred pieces". It moves Hessians and intensity tensors from fragments of up to about 100 atoms into DNA, RNA and peptides of thousands of atoms ([Andrushchenko & Bouř 2010](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1002%2Fchir.20872%22)). Its authors report that H-bonded helices "required larger fragments for the same accuracy" ([Yamamoto et al. 2012](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fct200714h%22)). Partial-Hessian analysis and the mobile-block Hessian still need the full molecule's electronic structure, so they are not assembly. One PHVA-plus-VPT2 study did treat PAH C–H stretches, "in close agreement" with full anharmonic calculations ([Hanson-Heine, George & Besley 2012](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.4727853%22)). That works because C–H stretches are local.

Our own E9 sits squarely inside this range. It rebuilt the ΔH of 182 substituted aromatics on 14 cores from the parent core's block plus the molecule's own Hessian columns within two bonds of the substituent, and reached **1.72 cm⁻¹ against a zero rule of 23.1** ([E9 pre-registration and result](../GoalGathering/notes/PreRegistration_2026-09-24_E9_Core_Transfer_Substituent_Probe.md)). The same table shows the catch. At two bonds, the parent block alone ("transfer only") gives **10.0 cm⁻¹**, and the probed columns alone give 18.7. The 1.72 needs both, and the probe half computes a quarter of the target's own columns at the high level. E9 therefore shows that ΔH is local on a substituted aromatic. It does not yet show that pure transfer from other molecules reaches a few cm⁻¹. It also says nothing about the fully fused interior of a large PAH, where no single parent contains the environment.

## Aromatic fragmentation stopped at energies and geometries

The machinery to do the PAH version has existed in one group for years without being joined up. AroBOROS (Noffke, Beckett, Li, Raghavachari 2020) fragments PAHs within MIM into **biphenyl and naphthalene subsystems that overlap by whole sextet rings**, so every calculation is on a real aromatic molecule. Errors fall below chemical accuracy only when the subsystems follow Clar's sextet structures. It was tested on 18–84 C, with a 132-C nanotube fragment, and only for **energies** ([Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.9b00566%22)). AROFRAG (Masoumifeshani & Korona 2024) treats "a sextet ring as a single unbreakable unit" and reaches a few millihartree on graphene-like structures, fullerenes and nanotubes, again for energies only ([Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.3c00875%22)). None of the citers of AroBOROS (6 in Europe PMC, 11 in Semantic Scholar) or of AROFRAG (4 and 8) computes a Hessian or a spectrum ([Europe PMC citations](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/MED/32119545/citations?format=json); [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1021/acs.jctc.9b00566/citations?fields=title,year,venue,externalIds,abstract&limit=100)). MIM's Hessian code and AroBOROS's PAH fragment generator come from the same group. That pairing is the obvious prior-art route to our idea, and its absence from the literature is the strongest evidence for the narrowed claim. A reviewer will ask "why not AroBOROS-MIM Hessians?", and that is the baseline a paper must beat or at least run.

The only fragment study of 2D π systems that goes beyond energies is Yeole and Gadre's MTA work. For 1D conjugated molecules the energy error is below 1 mEh. For small and medium 2D π-conjugated molecules it is "a few millihartrees", while gradients agree well enough for geometry optimisation. There are no Hessians, and none of the 42 citing papers adds them ([Yeole & Gadre 2010](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.3324702%22)). (One research note attributes this title to Nagata, Fedorov et al.; the Europe PMC record with DOI 10.1063/1.3324702 names Yeole and Gadre, and that is the attribution used here.) Thermochemists reached the same structural insight: Chan and Karton balance isodesmic reactions for aromatic hydrocarbons by splitting the target "into two halves with an aromatic overlapping region" ([Chan & Karton 2021](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1039%2Fd1cp01659h%22)).

The Pisa–Bologna line moved from fragments for structures to topology-dependent corrections for vibrations over 2022–2026, and it is the competitor to watch. The Lego-brick approach assembles PAH and PANH equilibrium structures from semi-experimental fragment structures. It needs a template-molecule step for the change on joining and a linear regression for the linkages, and it reaches rotational constants within 0.1% ([Ye et al. 2022](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2022/cp/d2cp03294e)). Templating synthons of up to about 20 atoms cut B3LYP geometry errors by nearly an order of magnitude ([Di Grande, Lazzari & Barone 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:40548656%20OR%20DOI:%2210.1063/5.0255564%22%20OR%20DOI:%2210.1021/acs.jpca.6c00650%22%20OR%20DOI:%2210.1021/acs.jctc.4c00900%22&format=json&resultType=core)). None of the 34 citers of Ye 2022 applies the templates to force fields ([OpenAlex citing list](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W4293752094&per-page=50&select=doi,title,publication_year,primary_location&sort=publication_year:desc)). Three 2026 papers then come close to our idea:

| Paper (2026, all closed, abstracts only) | What the abstract says | Distance from our idea |

The first of these is the one that could sink the claim. If "fragment space" in 064104 means explicit high-level calculations on PAH pieces, transferred to larger PAHs and checked against a full calculation, our idea is published. The abstract suggests something else: parametrised topology classes, closer to a learned local correction than to fragment assembly. Its only numbers are rotational-constant errors near 1% falling by over an order of magnitude ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1063/5.0343929)). The JCTC paper's mode-resolved assembly is the nearest published form of a per-mode correction from pieces. The group's largest vibrationally treated PAH in the abstracts is C21 (sumanene), done as a full GVPT2 calculation with fundamentals 5–10 cm⁻¹ from experiment ([Barone et al., JCP 164, 114309](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F5.0313437%22)).

Transferring a correction from small to large PAHs has a long history, but as scale factors rather than Hessian blocks. In 1992, MNDO-SQM factors per coordinate type, fitted on benzene and naphthalene and transferred to larger PAHs, gave **25–30 cm⁻¹ mean error** (a figure from a search summary) ([THEOCHEM 1992](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/016612809280085Z)). Uniformly scaled DFT reaches about 10 cm⁻¹ against matrix fundamentals for 13 PAHs up to ovalene ([Langhoff 1996, NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20020040898)). Pulay-type scaling of B3LYP/6-31G* force constants gets below 8.5 cm⁻¹ on organic sets ([Baker, Jarzecki & Pulay record](https://opact.sdu.edu.tr/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-a361t-17ed34c164e42647afdf23e522ea1aa14cd67ecf3e933521eaadba52a7d0b13a3)). The newest variant is the "Ethereal AI" paper, now published. A linear SVR predicts a corrected frequency per band for B3LYP/4-31G against PAHdb experiment, with **MAE 5.07 cm⁻¹ against 10.41 for PAHdb's three-factor scaling** ([Bos et al. 2025, PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12750190/fullTextXML)). Its split is random over 465 bands from 80+ small-biased PAHs, so molecules leak between training and test, and it has no size test and no fragments. These numbers measure against experiment rather than against ωB97X, so they are baselines for the end product, not for the proxy. Under the project's rules, a scale factor is only ever the baseline, never an option.

## Machine learning learned from pieces, but never a large PAH's mode correction

The ML literature contains every ingredient but not the combination. AMONs (Huang & von Lilienfeld 2020) build a query-specific training set from the target's own small fragments, mostly of at most 7 heavy atoms, for energies, forces, charges, NMR shifts and polarisabilities. They never did frequencies, and they had no PAH ([arXiv:1707.04146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.04146v4)). Their polyacetylene test is the closest analogue to our size penalty. At fixed amon size the error grows with chain length. Amons of 3 acetylene units hold 1 kcal/mol up to 14 C, and 4–5 units reach chemical accuracy up to 28–30 C. Watson–Crick pairs needed amons of up to 11 heavy atoms because of "non-local effects through their conjugated moieties" ([arXiv:1707.04146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.04146v4)). That argues for the largest affordable pieces, 10–30 atoms, over amon-sized ones. GEMS trains a protein force field on overlapping top-down cut-outs plus small bottom-up graphs, but drops the fragments at evaluation ([Unke et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11809612/)). HIP predicts Hessians directly as symmetrised 3×3 atom-pair blocks, but every block comes from whole-molecule features with a 12 Å cutoff that covers every pair in training (Table 6), so it is not an assembly from pieces ([Burger et al., arXiv:2509.21624 v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21624)). Its size test (Sec. 4.4.1, Fig. 3c–d) is in the arXiv text: trained on molecules of at most 22 atoms, the eigenvalue MAE stays at about 0.03–0.05 eV/Å² on RGD1 (10–33 atoms) but rises roughly linearly from about 0.13 to 0.68 eV/Å², about 5×, on PubChem CHNO molecules of 30–100 atoms (read off the plot). "Relatively flat" holds only against autodiff Hessians, which rise from about 2 to 8.5. The test reports no cm⁻¹ errors and no eigenvector overlap, and names no PAH. Its 5× is close to our own 4.4×. Domenichini and Dellago learned internal-coordinate force constants from local descriptors on QM7 and tested on QM9 molecules of 8–9 heavy atoms. Stretches came within 5–10 cm⁻¹, while low-frequency modes failed because "such vibrations are strongly non-local" ([Domenichini & Dellago 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16512)). Every transferable Hessian data set (Hessian QM9, HORM) stays at or below about 30 atoms ([Williams et al., arXiv:2408.08006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.08006); [Cui et al., arXiv:2505.12447](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12447v1)). None learns a difference between two functionals across molecules.

Only one paper tests frequencies (in cm⁻¹) from small to large PAHs against DFT, and it is barely a size test. Laurens et al. trained a Behler–Parrinello network (local atomic energies, 6.35 Å cutoff, Hessian of the whole molecule) on 11 PAHs of 18–36 atoms and tested on six others, all at B3LYP/N07D. The split is not by size and the paper gives no criterion: five of the six test molecules have 30–36 atoms, inside the training range. Harmonic RMSD was **18.3 cm⁻¹ on training and 22.1 cm⁻¹ on test molecules** (30×30 network). The one real size step, ovalene (46 atoms), is about 25.1 cm⁻¹, 1.37× the training error (read off Fig. 2). The anharmonic comparison leaves out coronene and ovalene, so no molecule in it is larger than the training set ([Laurens et al. 2021, arXiv:2010.13686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13686)). The paper is internally inconsistent about the test set (8 molecules in the abstract, 6 in the methods) and the total compared (17 on p. 2, 19 in the conclusions). It cites Kovács et al., ApJ 902, 100 (2020), not yet checked. Mai et al. ran a 3.8 Å-cutoff force field trained on PAHdb (≤ 50 C) on PAHs up to 216 C, but checked against DFT only up to 32 C and reported intensity RMSE, not cm⁻¹ ([Mai et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05120v2)). Tang et al. predict whole spectra from the 2D bond graph, binned at 18.23 cm⁻¹, normalised to the peak and scored as a spectral divergence (JSD), never in cm⁻¹. They trained on 1,570 PAHdb neutrals listed as at most 47 C, almost all at most 34 C (Fig. 7b inset), tested on 997 PAHs of 50–100 C, and found errors rising above N_C = 40. They name scarce large-PAH data, topology-only inputs and "long-range couplings" as possible causes, but their Fig. 7a shows the localised high-frequency region degrading most (about 10–15× in JSD, against about 3× for the low-frequency region), which points to training density. Nothing is assembled from pieces ([Tang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12560)).

Our own size test belongs in this company and is harder than Laurens's. The transformer predicts a per-normal-mode correction from B3LYP/6-31G* to ωB97X (the proxy) and was trained on molecules of at most about 30 atoms. It takes coronene, peropyrene, ovalene and HBC (36, 40, 46 and 60 atoms) from **22.8 to 7.9 cm⁻¹ ω rms**. That error is **4.4×** its error on corpus-size molecules ([chain 42 read](../modules/05_support_predictor/out/read_chain42.md); [TASKS 199](../TASKS.md)). Adding six training molecules of 34–54 atoms barely moved it (g = 0.92). Laurens's test-to-train ratio of 22.1/18.3 ≈ 1.2× looks gentler, but their test molecules were barely larger than the training set (46 vs 36 atoms, with coronene in training), and their roughly 20 cm⁻¹ is a full B3LYP frequency from an MLIP, not a correction to it. The two numbers measure different things. The point that matters is that the astro-PAH ML literature hit the same wall we did and proposed more large-PAH data as the remedy. Our chain 42 suggests data alone will not do it. Nobody has proposed assembling the correction from pieces that contain the large molecule's interior environments.

The zero rules of E9 (23.1) and of chain 42 (22.8) happen to be almost equal, which allows a rough comparison. This is our inference, not a published result. The network's 7.9 cm⁻¹ on the large four sits between E9's transfer-only 10.0 and its transfer-plus-probe 1.72. E9's transfer-only number understates what overlapping pieces could do, since E9's parent core contains no information on the substituent region, whereas overlapping PAH pieces would cover every atom of the target. Still, a pieces route that aims below 3 cm⁻¹ on 34–60 atoms will probably need either pieces that contain coronene-like interior carbons, or a few high-level probe columns of the large molecule itself, as E9 and the JPCL 2026 curvature-probing scheme both use.

## Seven pitfalls the literature has already mapped

The pitfalls are documented, but none has been measured on a large-PAH Hessian. The first is **2D conjugation**. In fragmented DFT, energy errors for 2D π systems are several millihartree, against below 1 mEh for 1D ([Yeole & Gadre 2010](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1063%2F1.3324702%22)). Delocalisation error also "poisons" density-functional many-body expansions ([Broderick & Herbert 2024](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/sc/d4sc05955g)). The second is **Clar sextets**. AroBOROS reaches chemical accuracy only with Clar-consistent subsystems, and AROFRAG refuses to break a sextet ([Noffke et al. 2020](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.9b00566%22); [Masoumifeshani & Korona 2024](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.3c00875%22)). An H-capped PAH piece is a different aromatic molecule with its sextets moved, so its high-minus-low difference need not equal the embedded one. The third is **H-capping and link atoms**. SMFA forbids cutting multiple bonds ([Collins 2014](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/16184/1/01_Collins_Molecular_forces%2c_geometries%2c_2014.pdf)). By its own rule every PAH C–C bond counts as multiple, so SMFA cannot fragment a PAH at all (our reading of the rule). Barone reports that the link-atom approximation "may leave a residual error concentrated in the harmonic force field" at delicate boundary bonds, and fixes it with a transferable local correction ([Barone et al. 2026](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Facs.jctc.6c00816%22)). Lego-brick needed a template correction even for geometries.

The fourth pitfall, **delocalised low-frequency modes**, is the one most likely to set our floor. Fragment Hessians work for peptides because modes localise "within individual chemical moieties" ([Herbert 2019](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)). In a PAH the moiety is the whole π framework. Local Hessian models fail on low-frequency modes for this reason ([Domenichini & Dellago 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16512)). This fits the project's own finding that out-of-plane and low modes are the hard family. The fifth is **long-range couplings**: errors rise with conjugation length at fixed piece size ([Huang & von Lilienfeld](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.04146v4)). The rise above 40 C in a PAH spectrum model is not evidence for this: there the localised high-frequency region degrades most, which points to scarce training data ([Tang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12560), Fig. 7a). The MIM2-over-MIM1 gain shows where the remedy lies ([Jose & Raghavachari 2015](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1021%2Fct501026m%22)). In a ΔH scheme the full B3LYP Hessian is that low-level layer and carries the long-range part, so only the difference has to be local. Whether ΔH is local in a fused interior is the hypothesis to test. The sixth is **charge delocalisation in cations**. GEBF fails when fragments are forced to carry integer charges that are really fractional ([Li et al. 2018](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1039%2Fc8cp00513c%22)), and DCMB avoids caps precisely for "charged and/or delocalized systems" ([Wu & Xu 2012](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI%3A%2210.1002%2Fjcc.22973%22)). A PAH radical cation's hole spreads over the framework, so pieces would need fractional charge or a full-molecule layer. No study of open-shell fragment Hessians of conjugated systems was found. The seventh is **geometry and stationarity**. Grafted MTA frequencies are taken at a geometry that is not quite stationary at the frequency level ([Herbert 2019](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/herbert.44/reprints/JCP_151_170901.pdf)). Corpus pieces have their own optimised bond lengths, which differ from those of a coronene interior. Assembled CCT Hessians can also produce spurious imaginary frequencies (attribution uncertain, from a search snippet).

Two further hazards bear on the reference calculations rather than on the assembly. LNO-CCSD(T) gradients now exist, but loose cutoffs produce PES discontinuities ([arXiv:2404.03129](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03129v3)), which matters for any finite-difference Hessian used as a full-molecule benchmark. For the end product, Lemmens, Rijs and Buma measured jet-cooled IR spectra of **exactly our four large PAHs** (coronene, peropyrene, ovalene, HBC). Harmonic theory is good at 100–1000 cm⁻¹, falls short at 1600–2000 and 2950–3150 cm⁻¹, and GVPT2 gives "large and unrealistic" shifts in the CH out-of-plane region for large PAHs ([Lemmens, Rijs & Buma 2021](https://dare.uva.nl/id/651db345-cc4a-45f2-bd19-71c5ac331a12)). The harmonic correction pays off mainly above 1000 cm⁻¹, and an experimental check exists for all four targets.

## A defensible claim, and the closed full texts it waits on

The genuinely new part is a combination. Each element has precedent alone, and no paper joins them. The correction is high-minus-low (ωB97X, later CC, minus B3LYP), not a Hessian at one level. The targets are fused PAHs of 34–60 atoms, not peptides or clusters. The pieces are **reusable corpus molecules**, closed-shell aromatics in their own right that are computed once and used for many targets. They are not capped fragments cut fresh from each target, as in MTA, MIM and CCT, and not per-topology parameters, as appears to be the case in 064104. The output is a per-normal-mode correction of the whole molecule, which needs a rule for modes that span pieces. The literature has no general solution for that rule beyond Barone's two-fragment mode-resolved combination. Finally, the pieces can be ML-predicted rather than computed, which adds one transfer step that no fragment paper takes. The benchmark against the full-molecule ΔH on all four Lemmens molecules would itself be a first for fused PAHs.

A wording that survives this evidence:

> Fragment-assembled Hessians, including high-minus-low corrections added to a cheap full-molecule Hessian (MIM2, grafted MTA, N-body:many-body CCSD(T):MP2), are established for peptides, proteins, carbohydrates and molecular clusters, within a few cm⁻¹ of the full calculation; sextet-preserving PAH fragmentation (AroBOROS, AROFRAG) is established for energies, and fragment templates (Lego brick) for PAH geometries. In a targeted search of this literature (November 2026) we found no study that assembles the vibrational correction of a fused PAH of 34–60 atoms from overlapping PAH pieces of 10–30 atoms, reused across targets, and benchmarks it against the full-molecule calculation.

"Never been tried" should not appear. "To our knowledge" is acceptable only after the reading below. Before any public statement, the following must be read. Items 1 and 2 decide the claim; the rest close smaller risks.

## Conclusion

The search changes what the hard part of the idea is. The assembly algebra is not new, and the evidence says it works within a few cm⁻¹ wherever modes are local. What nobody has measured is whether the high-minus-low difference stays local inside a fused π sheet. Every published fragment-Hessian success rests on locality that peptides and clusters have and PAH interiors may lack. The experiment that settles this is therefore also the publishable result. It is the decay of ΔH blocks with bond distance in coronene and ovalene, read against the full-molecule ΔH, with Clar-consistent pieces and with naive capped ones. It would answer the 2D-conjugation and low-mode objections directly, and it would rank the pieces route against chain 42's 7.9 cm⁻¹ on the same four molecules.

Two things follow for the plan. AroBOROS-style sextet-preserving pieces are the baseline any design must include, since a reviewer will name them first. And E9's split result (10.0 from transfer alone, 1.72 with probe columns) suggests that a hybrid will reach the 3 cm⁻¹ licence bar sooner than pure transfer. The hybrid would combine overlapping corpus pieces for the bulk of ΔH with a few high-level curvature probes on the large molecule for the delocalised modes, a design that Barone's JPCL 2026 probing paper does not cover across molecules. The Barone group is one paper away from this idea. The full text of 064104 is the most valuable thing to read this month.
