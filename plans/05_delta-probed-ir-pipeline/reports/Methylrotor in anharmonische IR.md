# Check the coordinates before decoupling the methyl rotor

The methyl group of 4-methyl-1H-imidazole is a hindered rotor, not a free one. The literature points to the coordinate system, not perturbation theory itself, as the main source of its trouble in VPT2. In rectilinear normal coordinates a methyl torsion picks up huge cubic and quartic couplings, and these largely cancel in the final C-H stretch and CH3 bend fundamentals. In curvilinear (internal) coordinates the same couplings shrink by one to two orders of magnitude, while the bend and stretch fundamentals move by only 0-3 cm-1 on acetic acid ([Mendolicchio, Bloino, Barone 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9753597/)). Zeroing the torsion's couplings, the usual fix for near-free methyl tops in methylated PAHs ([Mackie et al. 2018](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/cp/c7cp06546a)), removes one side of that cancellation. That probably explains why our decoupling moves two bends by up to 5.9 cm-1, which is above our 3 cm-1 accuracy line (project records). No published experiment can settle a 6 cm-1 rotor effect on the bends for a molecule in our barrier range: I found no jet-cooled CH3 bend assignment for any small methyl aromatic, and B3LYP/6-31G*'s own errors are larger than the effect. The test that decides the question is therefore one we can run ourselves at no compute cost: re-express the quartic force field we have already computed in curvilinear coordinates, and see whether the bends land on the coupled or on the decoupled rectilinear values. A 1D relaxed torsion scan, checked against the microwave barrier, comes second at about 12 Hessian-equivalents. Variational routes come last; they cost tens to thousands of server-hours from 30 atoms up.

## A 317 cm-1 barrier makes this a bound, anharmonic oscillator

Microwave spectroscopy gives 4-methylimidazole a threefold barrier of **V3 = 317.20 cm-1** and its tautomer 5-methylimidazole **V3 = 386.001 cm-1**. 1-methylimidazole has **185.104 cm-1** and 2-methylimidazole **122.7529 cm-1** ([Gougoula et al. 2019, via IDEALS](https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/113564)). Toluene sits at the opposite extreme, with a pure sixfold barrier of **V6 = 4.8378 cm-1** ([core.ac.uk 52919931](https://core.ac.uk/download/52919931.pdf); which of two sources the number belongs to is not yet checked). Other hindered rotors near our range are 3-methylpyrrole at **V3 = 245.92 cm-1** ([HAL hal-03183059](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03183059)) and 2-methylthiophene at **V3 ≈ 197.7 cm-1** ([HAL hal-03181939](https://hal.u-pec.fr/hal-03181939v1/document)).

The rest of this paragraph is inference. Take a methyl internal-rotation constant of F ≈ 5.5 cm-1 (a typical value, not read from the 4-methylimidazole paper). A pure V3 cosine then gives a harmonic torsion of 3·√(V3·F) ≈ **125 cm-1** at the measured barrier. Our B3LYP/6-31G* harmonic torsion of **108.0 cm-1** (project records) corresponds to V3 ≈ **235 cm-1**, so B3LYP probably underestimates the barrier by about a quarter. With the zero-point torsional level near 60 cm-1 and the first excited level well under the barrier, the states that carry the stretch and bend fundamentals stay inside one well. In this regime the torsion is a real, if strongly anharmonic, oscillator, and VPT2 can in principle describe it. The "quasi-free rotor" argument that justified zeroing in 9-methylanthracene ([Maltseva et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.01497)) and the near-free tops of toluene and 4-picoline does not carry over to our molecule. For toluene, decoupling is almost certainly right. For a 300 cm-1 rotor it needs a test.

Our own numbers show the problem (project records, 3 Nov 2026, TASKS 156 (c)). In rectilinear semi-numerical VPT2 (step 0.10, grids 75/302 and 99/590), the torsion's pairs with the methyl C-H stretches carry the largest disagreement between our two routes: **5.63 → 5.26 cm-1** between grids, with a share of 0.93. That signature means large amplitude, not grid noise. The torsion's own quartic constant is **φ_iiii ≈ 19,600 cm-1**. Decoupling the torsion (zeroing every coupling of modes below 120 cm-1) moves the fundamentals in the window by up to **5.9 cm-1** (harmonic modes at 1507.5 and 1521.1 cm-1; rms 1.6 cm-1). For comparison, our two quartic routes differ by **0.43 cm-1**. So the choice of rotor policy is the largest single uncertainty on these two modes. It is larger than the 3 cm-1 per-family accuracy line.

## Rectilinear coordinates, not VPT2, inflate the torsion's couplings

Published methods fall into four families. **Decoupling**, or reduced-dimensionality VPT2, zeroes every cubic and quartic constant that carries the torsion index. Mackie et al. did this for 9-methyl- and 9,10-dimethylanthracene because of "known issues" with low-barrier methyl modes ([Mackie et al. 2018](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/cp/c7cp06546a)). Experienced practitioners are cautious about it. Franke, Stanton and Douberly write that reduced-dimensionality schemes "seldom lead to higher-accuracy predictions for the high-frequency vibrations" ([Franke, Stanton, Douberly 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10284833)). Yang and Bloino state that large-amplitude problems "cannot be overcome purely within VPT2" and need hybrid schemes ([Yang & Bloino 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9761684/)). **Hindered-rotor models** solve the torsion separately on a scan with a Fourier potential and a reduced moment of inertia. In Gaussian this serves thermochemistry: for acetaldehyde's methyl it gives an entropy of 63.10 against 63.06 cal/(mol K) measured ([Mendolicchio & Barone 2025](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jcp/article/162/15/154114/3344543/Vibrational-second-order-perturbation-theory-based)). It does not say how the torsion perturbs the bends. **Hybrid schemes** keep VPT2 for the stiff modes and give the torsion a variational DVR, optionally with coupled transverse channels. Examples are Barone's TRINITY preprint ([Barone 2026, arXiv 2610.20488](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20488v1)), the 1D-DVR coupling of Baiardi, Bloino and Barone, which sits only in Gaussian's development version ([Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/chemistry/articles/10.3389/fchem.2020.584203/full)), and a 2D torsion-wag model for the CH3OCH2 radical ([arXiv 1905.09506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.09506)). **Curvilinear VPT2** keeps the torsion inside VPT2 but expresses the force field in internal coordinates.

The curvilinear evidence matters most for our case. For acetic acid at MP2/junTZ, GVPT2 in Cartesian-based and in curvilinear coordinates gives almost the same methyl fundamentals (cm-1, Cartesian / curvilinear / experiment): a-stretch A' **3083 / 3084 / 3051**, s-stretch **2992 / 2992 / 2944**, a-deformation A' **1450 / 1448 / 1430**, s-deformation **1380 / 1377 / 1383**, a-deformation A'' **1440 / 1437 / 1430**, torsion **85 / 85 / 93**. Stripped of its inter-mode couplings, the torsion becomes **-5213 cm-1** in Cartesian coordinates ("completely unrealistic") but **68 cm-1** in curvilinear ones. The authors conclude that in Cartesian coordinates the torsion's own anharmonicity and its couplings are of similar size, which makes it very hard to separate the large-amplitude motion from the rest ([Mendolicchio, Bloino, Barone 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9753597/)). Barone's preprint shows the same effect for methyloxirane at B3LYP/6-31G*, our own level of theory. The torsion-CH stretch coupling drops from **1460.7 cm-1** in Cartesian-type coordinates to **17.8 cm-1** in internal ones, yet the CH stretch fundamental moves only from **2916.19 to 2916.41 cm-1**. Intensities of mixed CH stretch states do depend on the representation (16.26 against 36.44 for one mode) ([Barone 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20488v1); a single-author preprint, not peer-reviewed).

This is my reading of those results, not a published result for a methyl aromatic. Our φ_iiii ≈ 19,600 cm-1 is the same kind of number as acetic acid's -5213 cm-1: an artefact of rectilinear coordinates that cancels in the coupled sums. Coupled rectilinear VPT2 therefore probably gives about the right bends. Decoupling removes the torsion's terms from the other modes' anharmonic constants without removing the partner terms they were cancelling, so it probably moves the bends away from the right answer, not toward it. Two things could prove this wrong. The 1507.5/1521.1 cm-1 modes may be mixed ring-stretch/CH3 deformations: they lie above the usual aryl-methyl deformation range, which is an inference in the notes that the eigenvectors should confirm. And the torsion may couple more strongly to bends than to stretches. Torsion-vibration coupling is widespread in substituted toluenes ([Aust. J. Chem. CH23122](https://publish.csiro.au/CH/CH23122)), and it is strong enough that adiabatic separation "does not apply" to toluene's methyl ([Glasgow eprints 37065](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/37065)). The measured couplings are to the methyl out-of-plane wag and to low ring modes, though, not to the 1400-1550 cm-1 bands.

The C-H stretch region carries a second, separate problem. Every source agrees that stretch-bend Fermi resonance with CH3 bend overtones shapes the 2850-3000 cm-1 bands. Sibert's local-mode model ([Sibert group](https://sibert.chem.wisc.edu/publication/fermi-resonance-effects-in-the-vibrational-spectroscopy-of-methyl-and-methoxy-groups/)) and VPT2+K with large polyads ([Franke et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10284833)) treat it, whatever is done with the torsion.

None of the codes found reads pyscf Hessians directly. Any route therefore either feeds our finite-difference cubic and quartic constants in by hand or re-implements the step. Gaussian's documented mode-selection keyword is `SelectAnharmonicModes`. No keyword called "ReadAnhMode" was found ([Gaussian Freq](https://gaussian.com/freq/)). CFOUR, Q-Chem, MULTIMODE and GAMESS were not checked.

The claim that a curvilinear re-transformation of our stored force field needs no new electronic-structure work is an inference. Transforming a quartic force field to internal coordinates needs the first to fourth derivatives of those coordinates with respect to the Cartesians, and these are analytic functions of the geometry. If the curvilinear normal coordinates agree with the rectilinear ones to first order, the new semi-diagonal quartic constants need only the old cubic, the old semi-diagonal quartic and the old quadratic constants, all of which we have. The coordinate-dependent kinetic-energy (G-matrix) terms still have to be added. That step is software work, not computing time.

## No experiment in reach resolves a 6 cm-1 effect on the bends

Jet-cooled methyl spectra exist almost only for the C-H stretch region. In 9-methylanthracene the symmetric and asymmetric methyl stretches sit at **2877.5 and 2933.8 cm-1**, and in 9,10-dimethylanthracene at **2885.2 and 2929.2 cm-1** (IR-UV ion-dip, below 5 K). The methyl bands there are at least 6 cm-1 wide, and VPT2 "cannot account for" a plateau linked to the free rotation ([Maltseva et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.01497)). For toluene there are Ne-matrix and liquid stretch spectra compared with VSCF ([Knaanie et al. 2016](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/infrared-spectrum-of-toluene-comparison-of-anharmonic-isolated-mo/)), gas-phase overtones modelled with a torsion-dependent CH stretch ([OSU abstract](https://molspect.chemistry.ohio-state.edu/symposium_53/Abstracts/p107.html)), and a consistent S0 assignment for toluene-h8 and -d3 whose table could not be opened ([Nottingham ePrints 3362](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/3362/)). I found **no jet-cooled assignment of CH3 bend fundamentals (1370-1550 cm-1) for any small methyl aromatic**, and no published comparison of VPT2 with and without the torsion decoupled.

Three arguments, all inference, limit what an experiment can decide for us. First, B3LYP/6-31G*'s own error on methyl modes is tens of cm-1. Even at MP2/junTZ, acetic acid's methyl stretches come out **32-48 cm-1** high and its deformations within **-6 to +18 cm-1** of experiment ([PMC9753597](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9753597/)). A measured band therefore cannot isolate a 6 cm-1 difference between two treatments at our level of theory. It can do so only at the level of our coupled-cluster anchors, or through differences such as isotope shifts. Second, room-temperature gas-phase band centres are uncertain by roughly ±2-5 cm-1 (the notes' estimate), which is comparable to the effect. Third, the gas phase holds a mixture of the 4- and 5-methylimidazole tautomers, which have separate barriers of 317 and 386 cm-1 ([Gougoula 2019](https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/113564)) and overlapping bands. A better-matched reference would be **1-methylimidazole** (12 atoms, V3 = 185 cm-1, no NH tautomer because the methyl sits on nitrogen) or **3-methylpyrrole** (V3 = 246 cm-1). It is still unknown whether either has assigned gas-phase or matrix bend fundamentals. The microwave barrier itself, though, is an exact reference for one thing: the torsion potential. It can tell us whether B3LYP's barrier is too low, as the 108 cm-1 harmonic value suggests.

## Zero-compute checks cost nothing; variational routes cost hundreds of hours

Plain semi-numerical VPT2 needs **2M+1 Hessians**: one at the minimum plus two displaced geometries per mode ([ORCA 6.1 manual](https://www.faccts.de/docs/orca/6.1/manual/contents/spectroscopyproperties/vpt2.html)). That is 61, 169 and 259 Hessians for 12, 30 and 45 atoms. Our measured pyscf B3LYP/6-31G* Hessian time (grid 75/302, 4 threads, server) fits seconds ≈ e^-4.94 · atoms^3.89 over 19 molecules of 12-20 atoms: about **114 s at 12 atoms, 4,000 s at 30, 19,500 s at 45 and 60,000 s at 60**. The values past 20 atoms are extrapolated. A 30-atom tetracene Hessian on an 8-thread server measured 4,419-4,489 s (project records).

The table below prices each treatment as new Hessian-equivalents (Heq), the single-job wall time that follows, and euros at €0.84 per server-hour with one job per server. The euro figure is an upper bound. Doubling the threads did not speed up the tetracene Hessian, so several 4-thread jobs packed onto one server probably use it better (inference). Counts marked [i] are inferences from the method's recipe, not published counts. I count a constrained optimisation as at most one Heq. That bound is mine: no published gradient count per relaxed-scan point was found. The ratio of energies per Hessian (15-50 at 12 atoms, 35-120 at 30, 54-180 at 45) is extrapolated from ADF's vendor claim that an analytic Hessian is 3-5 times quicker than 6N gradients ([SCM ADF docs](https://www.scm.com/doc.2022/ADF/Input/Vibrational_Spectroscopy.html)), plus a gradient costing 1-2 energies. It is an order-of-magnitude rule that still has to be measured in pyscf.

The fixed-count add-ons (torsion displacement, scan, adiabatic scan, 2D model) grow with the number of methyl groups, not with the number of atoms. Their cost per point still rises steeply, about 35-fold per Hessian from 12 to 30 atoms. Full VPT2 and grid VCI also grow with the number of modes, and grid VCI faster than linearly. Reduced VCI could in principle reuse the stored quartic force field and avoid new grids. But a Taylor quartic with φ_iiii ≈ 19,600 cm-1 is a poor potential for a torsion (inference), so the table prices new grid energies. For scale: one CCX53 at about €20 per day gives about 264 server-hours, roughly €220, between 4 November and the end of paid compute on 15 November (project records). Every 12-atom check fits in that window many times over. A full 45-atom VPT2 does not fit on one server.

Until rank 1 reports, the defensible interim policy (my judgement) splits by barrier. Near-free rotors (V below about 50 cm-1, toluene-like) are decoupled, following established practice. Hindered rotors such as 4-methylimidazole keep the coupled rectilinear values with a flag. The 5.9 cm-1 spread is carried as the stated uncertainty of the two affected bends. The threshold value of 50 cm-1 is my choice, not a sourced number.

## Conclusion

The question has changed. It is no longer "should the methyl torsion be removed from VPT2?" but "in which coordinates were its couplings computed?" For a barrier of about 300 cm-1, the published evidence implies that the large couplings are mostly a product of rectilinear coordinates and cancel in the coupled result. Zeroing them probably adds an error instead of removing one. That makes "decouple below 120 cm-1" unsafe as a general rule for hindered heteroaromatic methyls, though it stays sound for toluene-like free rotors. Our 5.9 cm-1 is better read as the size of the cancelling terms than as a correction.

The decisive experiment is computational and almost free, because the 61 Hessians it needs already exist. What it costs is software: a curvilinear transformation that, once built, applies to every methyl molecule in the corpus at no extra electronic-structure cost. The rotor-scan check against the microwave barrier costs under an hour per 12-atom molecule. Laboratory spectra are the weakest lever here. They come into play only after the coupled-cluster anchors, where our own level-of-theory error falls below the 6 cm-1 we are trying to resolve.
