# Specifications for a PC for this project

Written 25 Nov 14:43, at the user's request after the meeting with the supervisor (TASKS 191). One page: what the work needs, what
follows for the parts, and what a PC does not solve. Prices are estimates; nothing was ordered or priced today.

## What the work needs, from our own runs

| work | cores | memory | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCSD(T)/cc-pVDZ gradients for a reference Hessian, up to 193 basis functions | 2 workers × 8 threads | 2 × 10 GB given | the wave lists (`probes/anchor_wave_lists.py`: 2 \| 8 \| 10000) |
| CC dipole derivatives of the same molecules | 16 threads | 24 GB given | `probes/anchor_wave.sh` |
| MP2 basis step (density-fitted, cc-pVDZ and cc-pVTZ) | 4 threads | 0.7–4.1 GB measured, 8 GB cap | the MP2 lanes' logs, `probes/runspecs/anc*_mp2.json` |
| B3LYP and ωB97X Hessians of a radical cation, up to ≈ 300 basis functions | 6 threads | ≈ 11.4 GB peak by the fit | TASKS 121 |
| open-shell CCSD(T) gradient (cation reference) | 8 threads | under 12 GB for benzene⁺ | ledger, 26 October |
| the pipeline on one molecule (DFT, network, statement) | 6 threads | 9 GB bound | `probes/runspecs/pipeline_rehearsal_cinnoline.json` |
| training the network (292,546 parameters) | 4–8 threads, no GPU | a few GB | the training runs on the laptop |

Today's machines: a laptop with 8 cores and a 13 GB budget for runs, and rented servers of 16 virtual cores and 32 GB.

## The specification

1. **Processor: 16 cores, 32 threads, desktop class** (as the Ryzen 9 9950X of the September configuration). It runs what one
   rented server runs: two 8-thread workers. How much faster its cores are than the rented virtual cores is not measured.
2. **Memory: 128 GB as 2 × 64 GB, on a board that takes 4 modules** (256 GB later). 128 GB carries two workers up to pyrene
   (2 × 41 GB) and one worker for 1-cyanopyrene; coronene (177 GB per worker by the extrapolation) needs the 256 GB. Memory was
   55 % of the September price, so this line decides the cost; 64 GB would do for everything up to naphthalene-sized references
   and for all DFT work, and would not for anthracene with two workers.
3. **Scratch disk: 2 TB NVMe**, for the integral and amplitude files of coupled cluster and psi4's scratch; **data disk: 2 TB
   NVMe or larger**, for results (a single psi4 output of ours reaches 75 MB; the results folder is tens of GB — its size was being
   counted when this was written).
4. **No graphics card needed** for what we do: the network trains on the processor. A slot and a power supply that leave room for
   one (850 W) keep GPU-accelerated DFT open for later; not a requirement.
5. **Cooling and power supply for continuous full load** (a tower cooler of the NH-D15 class or a 360 mm liquid cooler), a case
   with airflow; runs last days to weeks.
6. **Linux as the operating system** (or dual boot): our chemistry stack runs under Linux; on the laptop it runs through WSL, with
   its own faults (the review hooks, a numerical difference in one routine found on 16 November).
7. **An uninterruptible power supply** is worth pricing: one power dip ends a run of weeks, although our runs resume.

## What a PC does and does not solve

- It replaces one rented server (about €0.30 an hour by our record of 20 November, so about €220 a month; we rent five now), and
  it removes the laptop's 13 GB limit for the cation data (≈ 1,150 laptop hours planned) and for the pipeline.
- It makes references up to about pyrene possible in weeks each. It does **not** bring the supervisor's 30–80 carbon atoms within
  reach: coronene alone is about four months per Hessian on one such machine by the rule. That range needs a computing centre
  (she raised SURF, TASKS 309) or stays with the 'learn on small, carry to large' route.
- Against renting: the September configuration cost €4,240.80, about nineteen months of one rented server at today's rate, if its
  cores were no faster. Worth it only if the project runs that long or the machine is wanted for other work.
