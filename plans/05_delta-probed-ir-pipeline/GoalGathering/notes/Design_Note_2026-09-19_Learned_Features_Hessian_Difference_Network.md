# Design note 2026-09-19 — letting the network *learn* the features: the correction Hessian as the object, the probe responses as the labels (written 19 September 2026, evening, at the user's request; no code, no compute)

*Why now. Two learning curves today (desk note of 18 September, §6) put the ring-in-plane family at 12.4 cm⁻¹ on the proxy and left it there when we added the hand-made environment descriptors, while the training error halved. Guessing features by reasoning stays on the programme — the user's instruction — but this note designs the other route: a network whose inputs are raw enough, and whose output object is physical enough, that the features are learned rather than chosen. It is a design and a staged test plan, not a result. Everything scored (per-family licence, laboratory margins, the baselines of §5 of the desk note) is unchanged: the network changes, the tests do not.*

## 1. Why the present network cannot learn features

The recipe's Transformer sees one token per DFT mode, and each token is already a handful of scalars we computed: frequency, a family label, C/H/N/O shares, a localisation index, today also environment-class shares. Whatever determines the correction but is not in those scalars is invisible to the model; attention between modes cannot recover what the tokens have thrown away. The training error of 2 cm⁻¹ against a held-out error of 12 says the model can fit anything we hand it and generalise none of it: the representation, not the capacity, is the bottleneck. A network learns features only when its input is the thing itself — the molecule's geometry and the mode's displacement field on it.

## 2. The object to learn: the correction Hessian, not the per-mode shift

Everything pipeline B measures is a linear functional of one matrix, the Cartesian force-constant correction **ΔH = H_CC − H_DFT** (3N × 3N, symmetric):

- the diagonal shift of mode i is δν_i ∝ L_iᵀ ΔH L_i,
- the coupling of modes i and j is L_iᵀ ΔH L_j,
- the response of an energy probe along pattern p is ½ pᵀ ΔH p (plus the measured quartic term),
- the gradient probe along pattern p is, to first order, **ΔH·p itself** — a full column of ΔH in the pattern direction.

So the natural target is ΔH as a function of the molecule, and the natural loss is the discrepancy between the network's ΔH_θ and the *raw* probe data, through exactly these projections: loss = Σ_probes ‖Π_p(ΔH_θ) − y_p‖². The network is then trained on what the deck actually measured, without first solving for the couplings, and every unknown of the recovery problem becomes a constraint on one shared object. Three consequences: (i) the symmetry-blocked structure, the locality of X9 (the correction beyond bond-graph distance 4 still moves a band by 10 cm⁻¹, beyond 6 by 0.4 — so a receptive field of ≈ 6 bonds is needed and sufficient), and the per-family behaviour all become *learned* rather than imposed; (ii) the couplings and the diagonal come from one model, so the C–H stretch family's "diagonal only" and the fingerprint's "couplings needed" (shape test, 17 September) are the same object read at different places; (iii) every molecule's deck, however thin, trains the whole matrix through its projections.

## 3. The architecture that can learn from that input

An **equivariant message-passing network over atoms**, of the family used for machine-learned potentials (PaiNN / NequIP / MACE-style: scalar and vector features per atom, messages along interatomic distances, rotations handled by construction), with a **pair-block head**: for every atom pair (a, b) within the receptive field it emits a 3 × 3 block ΔH_ab built from learned scalars times the outer products of the unit vector r̂_ab and the identity (the only rotation-covariant 3 × 3 forms a pair can make), with ΔH_aa = −Σ_b ΔH_ab to enforce translational invariance and symmetrisation to enforce ΔH = ΔHᵀ. The atom features are learned from element, geometry and — crucially — nothing we chose: solo/duo/trio/quartet, ring fusion, conjugation length are all functions of the neighbourhood the messages traverse, and the network will build whichever of them predict the labels. Size: 10⁴–10⁵ parameters, three to four message-passing layers (bond-graph distance 6 ≈ 3–4 hops in a cutoff of 4–5 Å). Recent equivariant Hessian learners exist in the machine-learned-potential literature (for transition-state search and vibrational analysis, 2024–2026); they are to be searched and read before any code is written, per the rule of the PI assessment (no novelty claim against unread work), and one may be usable off the shelf as the backbone.

A cheaper intermediate that already learns features: a **mode-conditioned atom transformer** — tokens are atoms (element, learned embedding, neighbour distances) *and* the mode's displacement vector on each atom, in a local frame; the output is the mode's correction. It learns which atomic displacement patterns matter, keeps the per-mode reading, but does not share one ΔH across modes and needs the couplings as separate labels. It is the fallback if the equivariant build is slow.

## 4. Where the labels come from, in three stages

**Stage 1 — pre-training on the proxy ΔH, which we already own in full.** Every corpus molecule carries *both* Hessians (B3LYP and ωB97X at the same geometry): ΔH_proxy is on disk for 45 molecules today, 3N × 3N each — for naphthalene 1,176 independent numbers, not 48 — and the corpus machinery makes it for 868 (layer A2) and 4,353 (layer B) more at ≈ 40 min per molecule on a rented machine. The pre-training loss is the full matrix (mass-weighted, translation/rotation-projected), so the network learns what a Hessian correction looks like as a function of local environment from thousands of examples, with no coupled-cluster cost. **Test 1 (decides whether features are learnable at all):** on the same 12 held-out molecules as today, read δν_i = L_iᵀ ΔH_θ L_i per family. The ring-in-plane family below 5 cm⁻¹ on the proxy where today's tokens sit at 12.4 is the win; no improvement means the proxy's ring correction is not a local function of geometry at all, which would itself be a finding about the proxy, not about the network.

**Stage 2 — fine-tuning on the coupled-cluster projections.** The labels are the deck responses of benzene and naphthalene as they exist and as they arrive (diagonal from energies, couplings from gradients, sealed per molecule), fed through the projections of §2 with a small learning rate and early stopping by held-out molecule. The structural difference between the proxy and the real correction (Käser & Meuwly's caveat, our §4 of the 18 September note) is exactly what the fine-tuning has to absorb; with ΔH as the shared object the first two molecules already constrain hundreds of matrix elements each. **Test 2:** leave-one-molecule-out between benzene and naphthalene against the sealed decks, per family, beside X18's type transfer (0.27 cm⁻¹ C–H stretch) and T-1's median rule — the network must beat both where it is to be licensed.

**Stage 3 — active selection.** With an ensemble of five seeds, the next molecule to label is the one whose predicted ΔH projections disagree most on the families that are not yet licensed, weighted by their margins (desk note §5.5). This is where the network pays for its own labels: it says which deck to run next.

## 5. What is kept, what is dropped, what is new

Kept: the per-family licence and refusal, the laboratory margins per scoring column, the baselines (zero, family median, X18 transfer), the reporting in cm⁻¹, the split by molecule and scaffold, seeds 0–2 (five for the ensemble), the corpus and its runner, the deck design of pipeline B. Dropped, if Test 1 wins: the mode-token Transformer as the primary model (it stays as the baseline in every table) and the hand-made descriptors as inputs (they stay as *diagnostics* — a learned model that cannot reproduce solo/duo/trio/quartet from geometry has not learned what a chemist knows). New: the network outputs a matrix with a physical meaning that can be checked against directly computed references (the same references that license the deck recovery), so a licence can be granted per family *and* per matrix block.

## 6. Cost and calendar

Stage 1 needs no quantum-chemical compute beyond what exists: a week of desk work (reading the Hessian-learning literature, a minimal equivariant pair-block model in PyTorch, the projection loss), then minutes of training on 45 molecules on the laptop at low priority or on a rented small machine, hours on a few hundred molecules from layer A2 (≈ €10 of corpus time per hundred). It can start before or after the 28th without touching the anchor run. Stage 2 starts when M2 delivers the first gradient decks (October); Stage 3 when three or more coupled-cluster molecules exist. The pre-registration for Test 1 (win: ring-in-plane below 5 cm⁻¹ on the proxy on today's 12 held-out molecules; loss: no better than 10; the mode-token model and both rules in the table) is written before the first training run, in the pattern of this repository.

## 7. What could make this fail, said now

1. The proxy's ring correction may be non-local (long-range exchange in ωB97X): then Stage 1 cannot get below ~10 even with perfect features, and the finding transfers to the choice of proxy, not to the network — a B3LYP-vs-hybrid-with-different-exact-exchange proxy (e.g. BHHLYP, which stage A already computes) would be the next pre-training target.
2. With 45 molecules an equivariant model may overfit as the Transformer did; the proxy corpus is the cure and is cheap.
3. The real correction may be less local than the proxy's (X9 says distance 6 suffices at naphthalene; larger acenes are unmeasured) — a receptive-field test on the first pentacene-class deck, not before.
4. Engineering: equivariant libraries are heavy; a minimal pair-block model written by hand is the plan, a library the fallback.
