# Research Note 2026-09-05 (R2) — Gas-phase mid-IR (6–15 µm) sources for pyrene, chrysene, triphenylene, tetracene, coronene

Question: does a published, publicly obtainable gas-phase IR spectrum exist in the 6–15 µm (≈650–1700 cm⁻¹) region for C16H10 pyrene, C18H12 chrysene, C18H12 triphenylene, C18H12 tetracene or C24H12 coronene, either (a) at/near room temperature, (b) jet-cooled / molecular beam, or (c) at a well-stated temperature with resolution < 4 cm⁻¹ and tabulated band centres?

Verification convention used below: "Crossref" = DOI resolved at api.crossref.org and title/venue/year/authors matched; "opened" = the primary page/PDF/full text was read; "snippet" = only search-engine snippets or a citing paper were read — such facts are marked as unverified where they matter.

## Verdict

No source satisfies condition (a) (room-temperature gas phase) for any of the five species, and none satisfies condition (c) as literally stated (resolution better than 4 cm⁻¹ over the whole 6–15 µm window with tabulated band centres). Condition (b), jet-cooled with tabulated band centres, is met for two of the five: **tetracene — yes** (Lemmens et al. 2019, A&A 628, A130: IR-UV ion-dip at FELIX, 5–18 µm, Table A.2 with 30+ experimental band centres, open access; FELIX bandwidth ≈1 % of frequency, i.e. ≈7 cm⁻¹ at 700 cm⁻¹ and ≈15 cm⁻¹ at 1500 cm⁻¹) and **coronene — yes** (Lemmens, Rijs & Buma 2021, ApJ 923, 238: laser-desorption molecular beam, 3–100 µm, T_rot ≈ 2 K, Table A1 with five band centres between 600 and 1700 cm⁻¹, CC BY). **Pyrene — partial**: no broadband cold 6–15 µm spectrum was found; what exists is one rotationally resolved band (ν68, in-plane CH wag near 8.5 µm; Brumfield, Stewart & McCall 2012, JPCL 3, 1985; slit jet, T_rot ≈ 25 K per snippet), a jet-cooled far-IR spectrum limited to 80–250 cm⁻¹ (Lemmens et al. 2020, JPCL 11, 8997), and hot (≈400–900 K) heat-pipe absorption spectra with published linear temperature coefficients for the main bands (Joblin et al. 1994, 1995; not opened, see table). **Chrysene — no** and **triphenylene — no**: the only gas-phase 6–15 µm data found are hot, low-resolution GC-IR spectra (NIST/EPA gas-phase library in the WebBook; Semmler, Yang & Crawford 1991); the only cold gas-phase data are the 3 µm C–H stretch (Maltseva et al. 2016) and, for chrysene only, the 80–250 cm⁻¹ far-IR window (Lemmens et al. 2020). No entry for any of the five species was found in PNNL/NWIR, HITRAN, GEISA, or a Zenodo/Kaggle deposit; the NASA Ames PAHdb laboratory library is argon-matrix only (15 K, 0.5 cm⁻¹).

## What this changes for the plan

Written conservatively; each claim is conditional on the grade above.

1. **Tetracene becomes decidable in the cold-gas frame, with a resolution caveat.** Lemmens et al. 2019 gives a jet-cooled 5–18 µm spectrum with a tabulated experimental band list (Table A.2, ≈30 bands between 548 and 1970 cm⁻¹, normalised intensities). Condition: the pipeline must accept band centres whose instrumental width is ≈1 % of the frequency (≈7–15 cm⁻¹ across 6–15 µm), i.e. a centre uncertainty of a few cm⁻¹, not < 4 cm⁻¹ resolution as in criterion (c). The table header typo ("C10H12" for tetracene) should be noted when the table is transcribed. Downloadable only as a PDF table (arXiv 1907.09351); no JCAMP/CSV.

2. **Coronene becomes decidable for its five strongest 6–15 µm bands only.** Lemmens, Rijs & Buma 2021 (CC BY) tabulates 854.6, 1132.1, 1208.1, 1306.4, 1607.1 cm⁻¹ at T_rot ≈ 2 K with the same ≈0.5–1 % FELIX bandwidth. Weak bands are absent; the 11.9 µm out-of-plane band (854.6) is the anchor. Hot gas-phase points exist for a temperature-shift cross-check (Joblin 1995; Kurtz 1992 at 620 K), but those two papers were not opened here and must be read before any coefficient is used.

3. **Pyrene remains inconclusive by construction for a broadband cold comparison, but two partial routes exist.** (i) One rotationally resolved band (ν68, ≈8.5 µm; Brumfield 2012) gives a single cold band origin of high precision — enough to test one predicted line, not a spectrum; the paper is paywalled and its band-origin value was not read here. (ii) The hot heat-pipe spectra of Joblin et al. 1994/1995 give band centres at 400–900 K together with linear temperature coefficients (order −0.2 to −1.4 × 10⁻² cm⁻¹ K⁻¹ for the 709, 750, 1096, 1435 cm⁻¹ bands, as quoted by Chakraborty et al. 2019). Extrapolating those to 0 K would give "gas-phase, cold-equivalent" centres with an uncertainty set by the linearity assumption and by the (unverified) spectrometer resolution; this is a model-dependent correction, and the plan should treat pyrene as "decidable only under an explicitly stated extrapolation model", not as measured. Chakraborty 2019 (condensed phase, 0.2 cm⁻¹, 14–723 K) can serve as an independent check of the anharmonic slopes but not of absolute gas-phase positions.

4. **Chrysene and triphenylene remain inconclusive by construction.** Only hot, ≈8 cm⁻¹ GC-IR spectra exist (NIST WebBook JCAMP; Semmler 1991), with unstated light-pipe temperature on the WebBook pages; no cold 6–15 µm data, no temperature coefficients. Nothing found in 2019–2025 extends the FELIX IR-UV work to these two isomers in the mid-IR (the 2020 JPCL paper covers chrysene only at 80–250 cm⁻¹). Any claim about the C18H12 isomer family in the 6–15 µm region can therefore only be made against matrix data (PAHdb, Hudgins & Sandford 1998) plus a matrix-to-gas shift assumption, or deferred.

5. **Family-level consequence.** Of the C18H12 isomers, only the linear one (tetracene) has a cold gas-phase mid-IR benchmark; the "isomer discrimination" goal for chrysene/triphenylene/tetracene cannot be closed on gas-phase experimental grounds and should be reframed as tetracene-vs-theory plus matrix-based comparisons for the other two, with that limitation stated in the plan.

6. **No public machine-readable cold spectra.** For both A-grade sources the usable artefact is a printed table, not a spectrum file; the pipeline must ingest band lists, and any "spectrum overlay" step for these species has to be reconstructed from the tables (Gaussian at the FELIX bandwidth). The hypothesised PAHdb "experimental gas-phase library" does not exist; the PAHdb laboratory library is argon-matrix only.
