Thanks again for #3384. Here is one more case, measured with #3384 (e799bb1 merged locally onto master 52f5737): the Hessian CPHF from
`solve_mo1` for a 22-atom molecule (C12H9Cl, RKS B3LYP/6-31G*, grid (99, 590), 66 right-hand sides).

#3384 detects the problem:

```
WARN: Krylov solver reached subspace convergence but true residual |aop(x)+x-b| = 3.016e-04 exceeds tolerance 2.2e-07. Falling back to per-root solve.
```

But the result hardly moves. The max |residual| scaled by e_ai, the same measure as in my comments above, is 8.1e-5 with #3384 and 8.2e-5
with 2.14.0. As far as I can read the code, the per-root solve runs to `fallback_tol`, which defaults to `1000*tol` = 2.2e-4. That is just
below the 3.0e-4 that triggered it.

The equations themselves do converge one right-hand side at a time. Starting from a batched solution, we re-solved the residual equation of
`solve_withs1` for all 66 columns at once with `lib.krylov` (tol 1e-9, max_cycle 50). The batched rounds stall:

| batched rounds | max residual |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1.7e-6 |
| 2 | 2.1e-6 |
| 3 | 2.1e-6 |
| 5 | 2.1e-6 |

Scipy's GMRES on each of the twelve worst columns alone (rtol 1e-10) brings them to between 2e-12 and 1.2e-11. The operator is deterministic:
two applications to the same vector differ by 1e-15.

So per-RHS solving is the right remedy here; the open question is its target. Would it make sense to use `fallback_tol` only to decide
whether to fall back, and then run the per-root solve to `tol`? Water B3LYP stopping at 1.5e-6 in my earlier table may be the same effect.
