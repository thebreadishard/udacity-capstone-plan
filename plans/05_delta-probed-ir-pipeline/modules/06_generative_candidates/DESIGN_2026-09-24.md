## 1. The generative task, and why this one

**Generate candidate molecules for the atlas** — new substituted and heteroatom PAH structures, as SMILES, from a Transformer trained on public
aromatic chemistry — so that the pipeline's "listed" layer and the website's request flow have a principled source of *what to compute next*, ranked
by how close a candidate sits to the families the learned layer is licensed for. The output is text (SMILES), the model a small decoder-only
Transformer (the rubric's third option; the same family as module 05's per-mode Transformer, which the report can contrast: there tokens were
vibrational modes and the task was classification, here tokens are SMILES characters and the task is generation).

Why not the alternatives: a GAN or VAE on molecule *images* would satisfy the rubric but feed nothing back into the project; a generator of spectra
would need the corpus, which module 05 used (dataset reuse is forbidden) and would produce synthetic spectra we then must not train on.

## 2. Dataset (the load-bearing rubric item)

**PubChem** compound records restricted to aromatic carbon frameworks: SMILES of compounds with ≥ 2 fused aromatic rings, elements in
{C, H, N, O, S, F, Cl}, heavy atoms ≤ 30, neutral, no isotopes — retrieved through the public PUG-REST / PUG-View interfaces or the bulk FTP
(Compound SDF), filtered with RDKit, and frozen as a checksummed CSV with the query, the date and the record count in `data/README.md`. PubChem
is public domain, existed long before the module, is real (deposited) chemistry — not synthetic, not AI-generated — and was **not** used by modules
02 (PAHdb computed library), 03 (PAHdb laboratory bands), 04 (matched pairs of both) or 05 (Hessian QM9 + the project's own corpus). Expected size:
tens of thousands of SMILES after filtering (to be counted at freeze; the rubric asks for size and scope in the report).

Held out by scaffold (Murcko), as module 05 did by core, so that novelty is measured against structures the model never saw.

Fallback if the PubChem retrieval is impractical on the day: ChEMBL's aromatic subset (also public, CC BY-SA) — same filters; the choice is recorded
before training.

## 3. Model and training

A character-level decoder-only Transformer (4 layers, 4 heads, d = 256, ≈ 3 M parameters; PyTorch, own code — no pretrained weights, so the
"student's own model runs" requirement is met literally), trained with next-token cross-entropy, AdamW, cosine schedule, dropout 0.1, on the
frozen CSV; training curves logged per epoch (loss, validity of 200 samples at temperature 1.0). Optional conditioning tokens at the sequence
start: ring count class and heteroatom set, so the atlas can ask for "three rings, one nitrogen". Compute: one CPU-day at most, or an hour on a
rented server after 28 September; nothing runs on the anchor laptop before the anchor is read.

## 4. Evaluation (pre-registered before training; numbers fixed then)

- **Validity** (RDKit parses), **uniqueness** (among 10,000 samples), **novelty** (not in training; scaffold not in training) — the standard trio,
  with citations to the benchmark literature (MOSES / GuacaMol) for the definitions.
- **Distribution match**: ring-count, heavy-atom-count and heteroatom histograms of samples against the held-out set (Wasserstein distances).
- **Project fit**: fraction of valid novel samples inside the corpus's licensed families (planar, ≤ 30 heavy atoms, corpus element set) — the
  number the atlas cares about; and the ten nearest training neighbours (Tanimoto) of five hand-picked samples, shown as depictions.
- **Failure cases** shown, not hidden: invalid strings, duplicates of training molecules, non-planar or charged outputs, mode collapse under
  conditioning.
- Predictions (to be fixed at pre-registration): validity ≥ 0.85, uniqueness ≥ 0.95, novelty ≥ 0.5 at temperature 1.0; conditioning obeyed in
  ≥ 0.8 of samples; family fit 0.3–0.6.

## 5. Ethics and responsible use (rubric: at least one concern, tied to our data/model/outputs)

- **Misuse and dual use**: a generator of aromatic structures is a generator of candidates, not of syntheses; PAHs include carcinogens and
  environmental pollutants, so outputs are labelled as spectroscopic targets, no synthesis routes or property predictions toward toxicity are
  produced, and the released model card says so.
- **Creative ownership and attribution**: training data are deposited public-domain records (PubChem); generated SMILES are not attributed to
  depositors; the model does not memorise-and-return depositor records (memorisation rate is measured and reported).
- **Bias**: PubChem over-represents what chemists have made or patented; the generator inherits that (measured by the distribution match against
  an astrochemically motivated list such as PAHdb's species list, used only as a *comparison* set, never as training data).
- **Societal impact**: modest and positive — cheaper triage of which molecules to compute for astronomical spectroscopy; the report says what it
  does not claim.
