## 1. Industry and problem (Task 1)

**Industry.** Laboratory astrophysics and molecular spectroscopy as a *data service*: the people who interpret JWST's infrared spectra of interstellar
matter do it against databases of molecular spectra (NASA's PAHdb is the reference for the aromatic families), and every entry in such a database is
either a laboratory measurement or a computed spectrum whose accuracy the user has to guess. The problem: **a computed infrared spectrum with a stated,
measured accuracy and a stated price**, for molecules that no laboratory has measured, delivered to a scientist as a certificate or as an honest refusal.

**Why AI belongs here.** The accurate method (coupled cluster) is unaffordable per molecule at the sizes that matter; the cheap method (DFT) is
affordable and systematically wrong. The project's thesis is a learned correction layer trained on a small set of expensive anchors (module 05), fed by a
corpus whose growth is planned by a generative candidate source (module 06) and a measurement plan (the standout proposer), run by an agentic steward
that enforces budgets and gates (module 07), and scored against laboratory data (module 03) and calibrated cheap columns (module 04). No single technique
does this; the *system* is the contribution.

**Constraints and risks.** Cost per label (measured: 4,200 s per neutral energy at DZ tight on the laptop, 34,411 s per naphthalene⁺ energy on a rented
16-core machine); licences that the learned layer must earn per family before its numbers may be shown as predictions; the temptation to present a
certified-looking spectrum for a molecule that is not certified; coverage bias (the corpus is neutral, small, aromatic); compute cost and energy.

reachable within the cap? is the layer licensed for its families?) → either a run on a rented server (the corpus deck, the anchor deck with the learned
order and the stop rule) whose outputs flow back through the same export, or a **refusal naming the rung, cap or gate** → the certificate: spectrum +
per-band error budget + cost record + provenance, signed by release and commit.

**Boundaries of capability and responsibility.** The system never shows a number without its source; a rung not reached shows "—" and why; the
learned layer's numbers appear only for families whose licence was earned on the registered learning curve; a request the gate refuses gets the
reason and the price of the missing step, not a guess. The system does not claim laboratory accuracy for anything not validated by module 03.

**Assumptions and trade-offs.** Static first (zero client JavaScript by default; the interactive parts are islands) — trades live queries for
reproducibility and cost; the request service is the one dynamic component and is designed so that everything else stays static. Per-job provisioning of
rented servers trades latency (minutes to hours) for a hard budget cap. The certificate carries uncertainty bands rather than single numbers, which
trades visual simplicity for honesty.

## 6. Ethics and responsible use (Task 6 §, rubric)

- **Transparency and accountability:** every number links to its file; the ledger is public; a certificate names release, commit, deck and
  pre-registration; corrections are dated addenda, never silent edits.
- **Misuse:** the largest risk is a certified-looking spectrum being cited for a molecule whose rung was never reached — the design answer is the refusal
  as a first-class output and the ladder on every page; predictions from the learned layer are shown only where the licence exists and always as a band.
- **Bias and coverage:** the corpus is neutral, small, aromatic; the atlas states what is absent (charge filter disabled with the reason; empty states
  say what is true) and the paper says whose spectra the system cannot serve yet.
- **Cost and energy:** the cost record makes the compute visible per certificate; the budget cap is enforced by the steward, not by goodwill.
- **Data and privacy:** no personal data; requests through OAuth carry only what the queue needs; no trackers or cookies on the site.
