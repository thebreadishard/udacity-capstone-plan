## 1. The conjecture, restated with its scope made explicit

**Conjecture T3 (Orientation, dated addition 2026-09-12).** For a family (M_n) of closed-shell π-conjugated molecules with bond graphs of maximum degree ≤ 3, DFT HOMO–LUMO gaps bounded below by γ > 0, and correlated and DFT energies computed in a fixed local basis, the atom-pair blocks of Δ₂ = H_CC − H_DFT satisfy ‖Δ₂[A,B]‖ ≤ C·e^{−α·d(A,B)}, d the bond-graph distance, C and α independent of n, α ∝ γ to first order.

Two remarks fix what the conjecture does and does not say.

- **It is a statement about each side, not about the difference.** If the CC Hessian and the DFT Hessian each decay in d(A,B) with constants (C₁, α₁) and (C₂, α₂), their difference decays with (C₁ + C₂, min α). T3 as written follows from two one-sided statements and gains nothing from the subtraction. The stronger and more useful claim — that the *correction* decays faster than either Hessian — is a separate conjecture, T3′ in §4, and it is the one plan 05 would profit from.
- **It is asymptotic in n, and the plan's sequence violates its gap hypothesis.** PAH gaps shrink with size (the acenes' towards zero, Hachmann et al. 2007, S3 note; the flakes' more slowly). A family with γ > 0 uniform is therefore a *sub-family* — bounded length, or bounded size — and on the plan's actual sequence towards the 101–386-carbon bin the theorem's rate α ∝ γ degrades by construction. Benzi, Boito & Razouk's own §12 treats the vanishing-gap limit and finds power-law, not exponential, decay (their Theorem 10.1). The conjecture should be read with "α(γ_n) → 0 as the gap closes" built in; X7 (λ against gap) measures the slope.

## 3. The layers of a proof, with their status

**L0 — Bernstein's theorem (their Theorem 8.7, quoted):** for f analytic inside the Bernstein ellipse E_χ (foci ±1, sum of half-axes χ) and real on the real axis, the best polynomial approximation error on [−1, 1] obeys E_k(f) ≤ 2M(χ)/(χ^k(χ − 1)). *Status:* classical theorem; **absent from Mathlib** (X4 Loogle check: Bernstein–Weierstrass on [0, 1] and Chebyshev minimax exist, the ellipse theorem does not). Their §8.5 also proves a two-interval variant (Theorem 8.11) for the gapped case.

**L1 — decay of functions of a sparse matrix (their Theorems 8.1, 8.4, Corollary 8.6):** a polynomial of degree k in a matrix respecting a graph G has zero entries beyond graph distance k; combined with L0, |[f(H)]_ij| ≤ c·e^{−α d(i,j)}, α = ln χ. *Status:* theorem for P = f_FD(H) and, with an ε, for the zero-temperature projector; **the combinatorial half is formalisable now** (T3a in §5), the analytic half needs L0.

**L2 — from projector/resolvent decay to correlation-energy decay** (*my sketch, not in the source*). The second-order (MP2) energy in a local orthonormal basis is a sum over occupied pairs of terms built from the two-electron integrals (ia|jb) and the resolvent of the zeroth-order Hamiltonian; the CC energy is the fixed point of amplitude equations of the same ingredients. The decay of E's *pair contributions* in the distance between the two occupied centres follows from (i) decay of the two-electron integrals over local orbitals (a separate, standard fact about Gaussian integrals — to be cited from a read text, not asserted), (ii) decay of the localised occupied and virtual orbitals themselves, which is the projector decay of L1 (a localised orbital is a column of a decaying projector, up to the localisation map, whose decay is preserved by their Theorem 9.2 for products), and (iii) for CC, an argument that the amplitude fixed point inherits the decay of its inputs — a contraction-mapping argument in a weighted norm, plausible but **unwritten**. The empirical shadow of L2 is X3b: MP2 pair energies at naphthalene decay with λ = 0.75 Å, with 2.3 % of the correlation energy beyond 3 Å. *Status:* new mathematics at CC level; at MP2 level a contained exercise given (i) and (ii).

**L3 — from energy decay to mixed-second-derivative decay** (*my sketch*). Δ₂[A,B] = ∂²(E_CC − E_DFT)/∂R_A∂R_B. For a quantity Q(R) = Σ of local terms whose dependence on R_A enters only through matrices supported near A (the derivative ∂_A H of a Gaussian-basis Fock matrix decays away from atom A), the resolvent identity gives ∂_A∂_B R(z) = R (∂_A H) R (∂_B H) R + R (∂_B H) R (∂_A H) R − R (∂_{AB} H) R, every term a product with an A-supported and a B-supported factor joined by decaying resolvents; their Theorem 9.2 (products of decaying sequences decay, "adapted from … Jaffard [71, Proposition 1]") turns that into decay of the mixed derivative in d(A,B), at a rate below the resolvent's. The ∂_{AB} H term vanishes for A ≠ B far apart, up to the basis functions' Euclidean overlap. *Status:* for the DFT (and HF) Hessian this is a theorem-shaped argument that nobody has to invent, only write; for the CC Hessian it requires L2's fixed-point decay to survive two differentiations — the genuinely open step. Theorem 9.2 as printed is for the banded index distance |i − j| with constants independent of n; the graph-distance version needs the bounded-degree ball count (at most Δ(Δ − 1)^{k−1} vertices at distance k) — the same lemma L1's formalisation needs.

**Summary of status.** L0, L1: theorems (L0 classical, L1 in the source). L3 for the DFT side: an exercise on top of L1 and Theorem 9.2. L2 and L3 for the CC side: new. T3 as stated therefore reduces to: *(new) the CC Hessian of a gapped bounded-degree family decays exponentially in bond-graph distance* — and that statement, not the difference, is what would have to be proved or falsified.

## 4. T3′ — the conjecture plan 05 would actually profit from, and its cheap test

**Conjecture T3′.** Under T3's hypotheses the correction decays *faster* than either Hessian: α_Δ > max(α_CC, α_DFT), because the long-range part of both Hessians is electrostatic and mean-field and cancels in the difference, leaving the correlation part, whose range is set by the pair-energy decay (λ = 0.75 Å at naphthalene, X3b).

X5 measured the block profile of Δ₂ but not of H_DFT itself, so T3′ has no data yet — and it is the one claim that would matter for plan 05 (a fast-decaying correction is what makes a sparse deck or a sparse product pattern possible at all). **X9, defined here (minutes, numpy, the stored benzene npz):** the atom-pair block profile of the mass-weighted H_low (DFT) and of H_high − H_low (the stand-in Δ₂) side by side, by bond-graph distance 0/1/2/3 and by Euclidean shell, on the same footing as X5; **losing condition for T3′ at benzene:** the ratio ‖Δ₂[A,B]‖/‖H_DFT[A,B]‖ does not fall with distance. X8's calibration already warns what "decays" must mean: measured on band positions, not on norms — X9 prints both, and its band-level column is the one that counts. Benzene's distance range (≤ 3 bonds) is short; naphthalene (≤ 5) is the first informative case, as for X5.

## 5. What Lean could hold, in order of cost

1. **T3a (combinatorial half of L1) — buildable next, after the anchor job:** for a matrix A that respects a graph G (A i j = 0 unless i = j or G.Adj i j) and any k, (A^k) i j = 0 whenever the graph distance exceeds k. Ingredients exist: `SimpleGraph.dist`, `SimpleGraph.Walk`, matrix powers, the pattern predicate already used in `MeasurementAlgebra.lean`. Induction on k through `Matrix.pow_succ` and the triangle inequality for `dist`. Estimate: hours, not days; no analysis. Corollary: a polynomial of degree k in A has the same property.
2. **T3b (the ball count):** on a graph of maximum degree Δ, at most Δ(Δ − 1)^{k−1} vertices lie at distance exactly k from a given one. Needed by both the graph-distance Theorem 9.2 and any O(n) truncation statement. Mathlib has `SimpleGraph.degree`, `maxDegree`, `neighborFinset`; the count is a finite induction. Estimate: a day.
3. **T3c (Theorem 9.2 in graph distance):** products of exponentially decaying matrices on a bounded-degree graph decay. Real analysis of a geometric series plus T3b. Estimate: days.
4. **L0 (Bernstein's ellipse theorem):** complex analysis — Cauchy's integral on an ellipse, the Joukowski map, Chebyshev expansions. Mathlib has the Cauchy integral formula on discs and Chebyshev polynomials with their recurrence; the ellipse version is a genuine formalisation project. Estimate: weeks to months; it would be a Mathlib contribution in its own right. **A cheaper route for the one function that matters:** the resolvent x ↦ 1/(z − x) has an explicit Chebyshev expansion with geometrically decaying coefficients (from the generating function of the Chebyshev polynomials, an elementary identity), so decay of the *resolvent* of a sparse matrix — which is what L2/L3 use — can be proved without Bernstein's general theorem. Estimate: a week or two of Lean.
5. **L2, L3:** not formalisable until the mathematics is written on paper; and the CC-side statement is new.

The Lean route's honest ordering is therefore T3a → T3b → T3c → the resolvent lemma, each a self-contained lemma with a name, none of which needs the physics layer Mathlib lacks. That is a different project from proving T3, and it is the part that can start.

## 6. What would falsify T3 or T3′ cheaply

- **X7** (λ against the DFT HOMO–LUMO gap, benzene → naphthalene → pyrene): T3's α ∝ γ predicts λ·gap roughly constant; a factor-two variation is the pre-stated losing condition.
- **X5 on the naphthalene tensor, tabulated by bond-graph distance** (the S1 note's proposal): a profile that does not fall from distance 3 to 5 bonds falsifies exponential decay at that size at the norm level; X8's band-level test says whether anything beyond one ring is droppable at 0.5 cm⁻¹.
- **X9** (§4) for T3′ at benzene: no falling ratio, no faster decay of the correction.
