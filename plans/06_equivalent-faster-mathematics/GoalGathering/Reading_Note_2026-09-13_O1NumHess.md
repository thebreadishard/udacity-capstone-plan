# Reading note 2026-09-13 — O1NumHess (Wang, Luo, Wang & Liu 2025): a Hessian from O(1) gradients, read in full, against plan 06's S5/lever C and T3

*Wang, B., Luo, S., Wang, Z., & Liu, W. (2025). O1NumHess: A fast and accurate seminumerical Hessian algorithm using only O(1) gradients. J. Chem. Theory Comput. 21(21), 10893–10909, DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01354; read from the open arXiv text 2508.07544v1 (11 Aug 2025; `Papers/plan06/O1NumHess_Wang2025_arXiv2508.07544.pdf`, git-ignored; downloaded 13 September with the user's permission). Plan 05 bibliography item 34. Quotations are from that text.*

## 1. What they do

- **Claim.** The complete seminumerical Hessian of a molecule from gradients at an *asymptotically constant* number of displaced geometries (≈ 40 for C₃₂ chains, a plateau of ≈ 100–120 gradients on the WCCR10 and S30L-CI sets), against 6N_atom for conventional double-sided differences — "the first algorithm … for calculating the complete seminumerical Hessian of a molecular system using O(1) gradients."
- **Structural assumption: ODLR.** Not sparsity but *off-diagonal low rank*: "the blocks of the Hessian that correspond to two distant groups of atoms have low rank", i.e. H ≈ H_local + H_lowrank (their Eq. 20). Their Fig. 1: the ground-state Hessian of n-C₃₂H₆₆ is nearly banded; that of the polyene C₃₂H₃₄ has "regular spaced non-negligible matrix elements far away from the diagonal" which for its T₁ and S₁ states "do not appear to decay with distance" — yet the 60 × 60 far blocks have low numerical rank while the whole Hessians are "almost full numerical rank".
- **Why (their §II).** Differentiating Hellmann–Feynman twice gives H = H⁽⁰⁾ + ⟨Ψ|∂H/∂ξ_j R ∂H/∂ξ_i|Ψ⟩ + c.c. with the resolvent R. An energy-scale split R = R⁽¹⁾ + R⁽²⁾: R⁽¹⁾ is an entire function of (H − E_I), so a convergent power series in a *local* operator — local (the same polynomial argument as Benzi, Boito & Razouk's decay theorems, our S1 note); R⁽²⁾ is carried by the few states close to E_I and is therefore **low rank**, with coefficients that are nonadiabatic couplings. Conclusions they draw: the Hessian "is not necessarily local"; "when there are other electronic states that are energetically very close … the locality of the Hessian tends to be worse"; but H ≈ H_local + H_lowrank always. They flag that the argument is rigorous only where Hellmann–Feynman holds (not with Pulay terms, not for non-variational methods such as coupled cluster), "however, the qualitative conclusions should probably still hold".
- **Algorithm (their §III).** (a) A local Hessian from a penalised least-squares fit of gradients along *many-atom* displacement directions, with a distance penalty on far pairs (van der Waals radii; one tunable Δr⁽¹⁾ = 1 bohr; far-range pairs beyond Δr⁽¹⁾ + 5 bohr set to zero), solved by GMRES/CG on a sparse system; (b) an iterative low-rank correction that makes the Hessian reproduce the gradients exactly, alternated with symmetrisation; (c) deterministic displacement directions: 3 translations, 3 rotations, the symmetric breathing mode (double-sided, to absorb the leading cubic anharmonicity), then locally complete "stiffest remaining local mode" directions from a Swart model Hessian, orthogonalised — random directions were 2–13× worse and gave 5–10 kcal/mol systematic free-energy errors. Negative eigenvalues trigger extra directions.
- **Accuracy (their §V).** Errors "only about two times" the conventional seminumerical Hessian's. In cm⁻¹ MAD against the exact Hessian at Δr⁽¹⁾ = 1 bohr, with exact (simulated) gradients: n-C₃₂H₆₆ 0.78; **C₃₂H₃₄ S₀ 6.9, T₁ 11.9, S₁ 6.1; maximum deviations 34–94 cm⁻¹** (their Table I); real gradients add ≈ 2 cm⁻¹. WCCR10: 26 of 30 molecules below 5 cm⁻¹ MAD; reaction ΔG MAD 1.18 kcal/mol. S30L-CI: largest errors "in molecules with very large conjugated systems". Faster than analytic Hessians for most WCCR10 molecules; an order of magnitude less memory.
- **Prior art they name** (records verified 13 September): sparse numerical Hessians by a 3.2 Å cutoff (Yang, Ma, Lu & Bian, *J. Phys. Chem. A* 128, 3024 (2024), DOI 10.1021/acs.jpca.3c07645; RMS ≈ 5 cm⁻¹); compressed-sensing Hessians from random displacements, O(log N) gradients (Sanders, Andrade & Aspuru-Guzik, *ACS Cent. Sci.* 1, 24 (2015), DOI 10.1021/oc5000404 — plan 05's item 24, applied to polyacenes); mode tracking (Reiher & Neugebauer, *J. Chem. Phys.* 118, 1634 (2003), DOI 10.1063/1.1523908); sparse low-rank PES approximation for anharmonic frequencies (Rai, Sargsyan, Najm & Hirata, *J. Math. Chem.* 57, 1732 (2019), DOI 10.1007/s10910-019-01034-z); partial and mobile-block Hessians. Code: github.com/ilcpm/O1NumHess and O1NumHess_QC (BDF and ORCA interfaces).

## 2. Against plan 06, point by point

1. **Same goal, different object, different accuracy class.** They recover the *whole* DFT Hessian to a few cm⁻¹ MAD (6–12 cm⁻¹ on conjugated chains, tens of cm⁻¹ maximum). Plan 05 needs the *correction* Δ₂ = H_CC − H_DFT to a band accuracy of 0.5 cm⁻¹, and plan 06's substitution scheme recovers Δ₂ **exactly** on a known pattern (X1c, X11, X14: 18 gradients at naphthalene under the symmetry prior). O1NumHess's errors on π systems are an order of magnitude above plan 05's tolerance; as a *replacement* for the deck it is not a candidate. As a *reference* for what "few gradients" means in chemistry it is the state of the art and must be cited wherever plan 06 says "O(1) gradients".
2. **ODLR is not what X1/X5 tested — a correction to the novelty note.** X1 and X5 found Δ₂ of full rank (30 of 30) as a *whole* matrix. O1NumHess says whole Hessians are full rank too; ODLR is about *far off-diagonal blocks between distant groups*. Benzene has no distant groups (graph distance ≤ 3), so **ODLR for Δ₂ is untested, not falsified**. The novelty note's sentence "a low-rank route has no support at benzene" is narrowed accordingly. The test is X15 (defined below).
3. **Their §II is T3's missing half.** Plan 06's T3 said "the correction decays with graph distance at a rate ∝ gap" and its audit found the gap hypothesis failing along the PAH series. O1NumHess's split says what remains when it fails: a *low-rank* remainder carried by near-degenerate states, with nonadiabatic couplings as coefficients. For PAHs this is exactly the π manifold (X9: the correction's range follows the π system). **T3 should be restated in ODLR form: Δ₂ = local part (decaying at the mean-field rate, X12's 0.25–0.29 per bond) + low-rank part (rank set by the number of low-lying π states).** That is a sharper, testable conjecture than T3, and it is what would license a few-gradient route for Δ₂ *beyond* the symmetry prior on low-symmetry PAHs. Their rigour caveat (non-variational CC, Pulay terms) applies with force to our object.
4. **Plan 05's dry-run deck already contains compressed sensing.** The multi-mode patterns and `fista_lasso` in `dryrun_dft_delta_recovery.py` are the Sanders–Andrade–Aspuru-Guzik idea in mode space; plan 05's proposal §2 cites them (item 24: anthracene needed 30 % of the Hessian columns). The lineage in the literature is Sanders 2015 → Yang 2024 → O1NumHess 2025 (OpenAlex citation chase, 13 September: 14 works cite Sanders 2015, among them Rai 2019, Yang 2024 and O1NumHess; 158 works cite Coleman & Moré 1984 and **none of their titles concerns molecular vibrations** — the colouring literature and the chemistry literature have not met, except through compressed sensing).
5. **Deterministic displacement directions matter** — their random-direction control was 2–13× worse. Plan 06's colour-class vectors are deterministic by construction (from the pattern); X1d measured their noise magnification (1.4–1.5×). Point of contact for M2a's cells 3–4 and for decision 34's item 1.
6. **Symmetry.** O1NumHess uses none (their displacement directions come from a model Hessian); plan 05's symmetry prior is what makes X14's count 9 instead of 48. On symmetric PAHs the two ideas are complementary: symmetry blocks first, ODLR within a block later.

## 3. What follows (bookkeeping)

- Novelty note: §3.1 corrected (ODLR untested, not falsified); §6 added (the aromatic-spectra question, with the citation chase); O1NumHess cited wherever "few gradients" appears (cost ladder lever C, X14 note, draft P25 §4a, M2 design).
- **X15 defined (S1/T3, desk, minutes on plan 02's Hessians; later on the naphthalene Δ₂):** singular values of the off-diagonal blocks between two distant atom groups (e.g. the two end rings of tetracene, opposite sectors of coronene) of the mass-weighted B3LYP Hessian, and the rank needed to hold every band within 0.5 cm⁻¹ when the far blocks are replaced by their rank-r truncation. Losing condition for ODLR at plan 05's tolerance: r must exceed a third of the block dimension. On Δ₂ at naphthalene the same, when the tensor exists.
- Ledger S1/S5: this paper is the reference point for lever C; T3's restatement in ODLR form is a dated addition to the T3 proof plan (to be written after X15's first numbers, not before).
