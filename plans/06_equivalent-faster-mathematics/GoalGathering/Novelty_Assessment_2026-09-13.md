# Novelty assessment 2026-09-13 — what in plan 06 has been done before, by whom, and what (if anything) is new

*Asked by the user on 13 September: "Is het al eerder gedaan, zo ja, door wie, zo nee, wat is de vernieuwing?" Answered under the house rule: every attribution below is to a record verified in Crossref or a text read in this project (the reading notes say which); where the honest answer is "not found in what we read", it says so, and it says what a real novelty check would still require. Crossref bibliographic search is not a systematic review; this note is a map of the prior art we know of, not a claim of priority.*

## 1. The short answer

Plan 06 has produced **no new way of solving the electronic problem** (its branch M): every idea on that side is a known theorem or a known method, and the plan's own contribution there is to have stated the conjecture (T3) that would matter and to have measured, on plan 05's data, that two attractive versions of it are false at benzene. On the cost side (branch C) plan 06 combines four known ingredients — graph-colouring recovery of sparse matrices, molecular symmetry, automatic-differentiation gradients, and the perturbative resonance criterion — around one object that, as far as we have read, nobody has treated this way: **the correction Δ₂ = H_CC − H_DFT as a sparse, symmetry-blocked matrix to be recovered from a handful of exactly-substituted Hessian–vector products**, with measured counts (6–7 products at benzene, 9 at naphthalene) and the observation that the count equals the largest irreducible-representation block. Whether that combination is new in the literature is **unverified**; the closest prior art (O1NumHess, 2025) found this morning reaches the same goal — a Hessian from O(1) gradients — by a different structural assumption (off-diagonal low rank) at an accuracy of 6–12 cm⁻¹ MAD on conjugated chains, an order of magnitude above plan 05's tolerance; read in full the same day (`Reading_Note_2026-09-13_O1NumHess.md`), it is the reference every plan-06 sentence about "few gradients" now cites.

## 3. What plan 06 adds — stated with its limits

1. **The object.** The colouring/substitution literature recovers *a* sparse symmetric matrix; O1NumHess recovers *the Hessian* under a low-rank assumption. Plan 06 recovers the **correction** H_CC − H_DFT, and measured two things about that object that the other two frameworks do not supply: it is **full rank** in mode space (X1: 30 of 30; X5: rank 30 in Cartesian space too), so a *global* low-rank route (S2) has no support at benzene — **but this says nothing against O1NumHess's off-diagonal low rank, which concerns far blocks between distant atom groups; benzene has no distant groups, so ODLR for Δ₂ is untested, not falsified (corrected 13 September after reading the paper in full; test = X15);** and it is **block-sparse by symmetry and nearly dense within blocks**, so the product count is set by the largest irrep block (X14: 9 at naphthalene) — 18 gradients against 474 energies, *exactly*, no approximation in the recovery. Whether anyone has applied substitution colouring to a correction matrix with the molecular symmetry prior is not something we have found; it is also not something we have searched systematically.
2. **The test that a sparsity claim must pass.** X8, X9 and X12 turned "small in norm" into "moves no band by more than 0.5 cm⁻¹" and showed that the two disagree by orders of magnitude for Δ₂ and for the DFT Hessian alike. Spectroscopists know that harmonic frequencies are a global function of the force-constant matrix; the quantified version for the correction (74 of 78 blocks needed at benzene; 1 % of the norm moves 14 cm⁻¹) is, as far as we know, ours. It is a method rule, not a theorem.
3. **An empirical fact against a natural hope.** The correction is *longer*-ranged than the mean-field Hessian (X9: its share rises from 2.7 % on-atom to 6.5 % at three bonds, 19–26 % for ring C–C pairs), and the mean field itself decays at a size-independent factor 0.25–0.29 per bond over nine PAHs (X12). One molecule and a DFT−DFT stand-in for X9; nine molecules for X12. We have not found this comparison in print; we have not searched for it beyond the reading done.
4. **P25's form.** The criterion is old (§2); using it as an *ordering* that the deck's own stopping rule judges — so it cannot make the deck worse — and measuring what it saves (19 of 47 pairs at benzene against a 17-pair oracle) is the plan's own small contribution.
5. **The formal certificates.** `MeasurementAlgebra.lean` proves the recovery theorems and the greedy Δ+1 colouring bound; Loogle found no such bound in Mathlib on 12 September. That is a formalisation contribution of modest size, not new mathematics.
6. **The frozen-space gradient as a design.** Zhang et al. differentiate spaces rebuilt at each geometry and report the resulting discontinuities and a benzene outlier; Mata & Werner froze domains for smooth surfaces without gradients by AD. Differentiating the frozen, transported spaces (M2's design note) would join the two; we have not found it done, and we have not built it.

## 4. What is *not* new, said plainly

No new solver; no new theorem beyond the formalised recovery lemmas; the conjecture T3 is stated, not proved, and its hypotheses fail asymptotically on the plan's own molecules; the cost ladder is arithmetic on measured numbers; the decision rule is project discipline. If the user is asked "what is the innovation of plan 06?", the defensible sentence is: *a measured, symmetry-exact, gradient-based measurement scheme for the coupled-cluster correction to a DFT Hessian, with the honest finding that the correction is the long-range object and that the scheme's value hangs on one unmeasured engine constant.*

## 4a. The user's sharper question: have these things been used before to find the spectra of aromatic molecules?

Answered from plan 05's verified bibliography and its recorded novelty search of 6 September (eight queries, `Relevant_Scientific_Papers.md`), plus today's OpenAlex citation chase.

- **Hessians of aromatic molecules from few measurements: yes.** Sanders, Andrade & Aspuru-Guzik 2015 (plan 05 item 24, read in full) recovered DFT Hessians of polyacenes by compressed sensing in a cheap method's eigenbasis — anthracene from 30 % of the Hessian columns to < 3 cm⁻¹, 1- to 15-ring polyacenes with roughly logarithmic growth. Plan 05's proposal §2 already positions itself against it, and plan 05's own multi-mode deck patterns are that idea in mode space. The chemistry lineage since is Yang et al. 2024 (distance-cutoff sparse Hessians) and O1NumHess 2025 (ODLR), neither on PAHs specifically though O1NumHess's hardest cases are conjugated chains.
- **Coupled-cluster quality for aromatic force fields: partly, and small.** CCSD(T)-F12b harmonic frequencies exist for benzene (Esposito, Fortenberry, Boersma & Allamandola 2024, item 45, read in full: the 5.45 cm⁻¹ B3LYP-vs-CC MAD is benzene only); hybrid quartic force fields with a CC quadratic part and DFT cubic/quartic parts are a precedent (Bégué, Carbonnière & Pouchan, item 27); the Concordant Mode Approach corrects a low-level Hessian towards CCSD(T) along normal modes (Lahm/Allen/Schaefer group, *J. Phys. Chem. A* 130, 3249 (2026), item 65, read in full) — full CC Hessians or full mode sets, on intermolecular complexes, "no multi-mode probing, no symmetry blocking, no local CC". PAH spectra at scale are DFT: PAHdb's scaled harmonic line, its anharmonic VPT2 line (Mackie et al., items 12), and the 2025 machine-learned line (plan 05's lines A–C).
- **A coupled-cluster *correction* to a DFT Hessian of a PAH, recovered from few local-CC energies or gradients: not found.** Plan 05's query 6 ("local coupled cluster harmonic frequencies polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons CCSD(T) corrected DFT") found reviews and no local-CC harmonic corrections for PAHs; query 5 (symmetry-blocked sparse recovery) found only Sanders 2015 and a full-Hessian symmetry shortcut in AMS. Today's OpenAlex chase: of the 158 works citing Coleman & Moré 1984, none has a title about molecular vibrations; the 14 works citing Sanders 2015 include no PAH-spectroscopy paper; O1NumHess has one citing work so far.

So the honest answer to "is it done before for aromatic spectra?": the *Hessian-from-few-measurements* idea was demonstrated on polyacenes in 2015 with DFT; the *coupled-cluster correction* probed cheaply for PAHs, with frozen local spaces, symmetry blocking and exact substitution, has not been found in print — with the same caveat as §5: our searches are keyword and citation chases, not a systematic review.
