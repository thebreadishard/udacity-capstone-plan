## 1. What "cheap enough" has to mean in numbers

The unit is **one molecule's correction Δ₂ at the anchor's level**: a deck of local-CC energies (or gradients) at fixed geometry. Plan 05's R1 deck for naphthalene is 96 + 96 + 282 = **474 energies** (proposal §3.2; 2M diagonal ± pairs for two functionals' worth of reference points plus the off-diagonal part under the symmetry prior). Measured per-energy prices:

| price per energy | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| benzene, cc-pVTZ, tight, frozen arm | 2,087 s = 34.8 min **m** | `results_m1/benzene_ccpvtz_tight.log` |
| benzene, cc-pVTZ, xtight, frozen arm | 76 min **m** (factor 2.1) | `results_m1/XTIGHT_READIN.md` |
| naphthalene, cc-pVTZ, tight | 11.5 h **m**, 19.8 GB | `results_timing/naphthalene_cc-pvtz_tight.json` |
| naphthalene, cc-pVTZ, xtight | **38.4 h m** (138,305 s of fragment solves, F = 3.34; measured 14 September 07:49; the 42–54 h estimate of 12 September was low) | plan 05 `Compute_Budget_2026-09-14.md` §row "F" |
| desktop (16 cores), naphthalene tight | *e* 3–5 h (core-count rule, never timed) | Budget desktop paragraph |
| Snellius thin node, naphthalene tight | *e* 1.5–2.5 h wall, 200–300 SBU | Budget Snellius note |

So the R1 deck as planned costs, on the laptop, **1.3–3 years**; on the desktop **4–16 months**; on Snellius **15–60 node-days** (P13 memo §3). "Many times" — say a training set of tens of PAHs at R1–R2 size — is therefore out of reach by any route without a change in the deck or in the price per point. That is the branch plan 06 has to move.

## 3. What this says about "running the pipeline many times"

- **With what is measured today** (A and B, both still to be confirmed at naphthalene): one R1-size molecule costs ≈ 300 energies. **On the laptop that is still ≈ 5 months** (300 × 11.5 h); it becomes 1.5–2 months on the desktop (3–5 h per energy, estimated) and 3–4 weeks on one Snellius node (≈ 1 week on four). So "from years to weeks" is mostly the machine, not the mathematics: the two levers together give a factor ≈ 5.3 (A: 3.34 **m** at naphthalene, 14 September — the 2.1–4.7 written here on 13 September was the model; B: 1.6), and A is the reversal of decision 20 (bias 0.47 against 0.11 cm⁻¹), not a plan-06 result. *(Clarified 2026-09-13 after the user's question.)* A training set of tens of naphthalene-size molecules becomes a cluster-months project, not a fantasy; a training set at pyrene size (72 modes, per energy an unmeasured 5–10× naphthalene) does not become affordable by these two levers alone. *(Dated addition 16 September: with plan 05's levers G and H of §5 — 69 min per naphthalene energy at cc-pVDZ tight **m**, ≈ 291 energies — the same R1-size molecule is ≈ 14 laptop-days at tight and ≈ 46 at xtight, and the pyrene-size set moves to the desktop's reach; §5 has the rows.)*
- **With gradients at small g** (C or D): tens of energy-equivalents per molecule; the pipeline could run across the corpus. This is why the measurement of g (§4's step "measure g in PySCFAD with frozen spaces") was the single most valuable number the project could still buy. *(Dated addition 16 September: bought on 14 September as far as the laptop allows — g = 2.84 / 3.20 at RHF / MP2, unmeasurable at the coupled-cluster level in 25 GB; decision rule §2b.)*
- **The network's own precondition is not a cost question.** Decision 32 requires (i) that the correction's range at R2–R3 be short enough for a transferable model to exist. X9 is the first data on that: at benzene the correction is the long-range object, its range following the π system; T3's audit *predicted* that the range grows as the gap closes with size — *dated correction 16 September:* X7 (14 September) measured the MP2 pair-energy decay length nearly gap-independent across benzene, naphthalene and pyrene (0.67–0.75 Å while the gap falls 44 %), so that prediction is not supported at this size; whether the *correction's* range (a different object from the pair energies) grows is still open and is what X9 on naphthalene measures. A network trained on Δ₂ blocks would have to learn a quantity that gets *less* local towards the sizes it is meant for. That does not close decision 32 — the condition is written to be measured at R2–R3 — but it sets the expectation, and it makes the family-level labels (band positions per family, P19's transfer test) the likelier training target than atom-pair blocks.
