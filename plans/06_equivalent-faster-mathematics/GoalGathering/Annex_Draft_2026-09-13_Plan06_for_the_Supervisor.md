## Where the side project stands (added 16 September 2026 — decision 42 of the main plan, after a cold read of this annex)

Four outcomes, in plain words, before the detail. **First,** none of the side project's own mathematics has produced a cheaper way to compute the correction: the correction is not sparse, not low-rank and not confined to the π electrons on the molecule where it was measured (benzene, on a DFT-vs-DFT stand-in for the coupled-cluster correction — the difference between two functionals used as a rehearsal, because no naphthalene coupled-cluster deck existed yet). **Second,** the one number the whole cost question turned on — how much a gradient costs relative to an energy in the production code — has been measured where it could be measured (2.8 at the mean-field level, 3.2 at MP2, 14 September) and found unmeasurable on the laptop at the coupled-cluster level (memory); the cheap-gradient route therefore has no laptop evidence and waits for a larger machine. **Third,** the levers that do change the cost live in the main plan, not here: a smaller basis set for the deck with a transferred increment (its probe is running, verdict about 22 September) and one energy instead of a pair for symmetry-antisymmetric displacements (confirmed 15 September) — together they cut a naphthalene deck from a desktop-year to weeks, if the first probe wins. **Fourth,** the side project's most useful finding so far is a per-family one (16 September): the correction to the C–H stretching modes transfers from benzene to naphthalene by local bond type almost exactly on the stand-in (0.27 cm⁻¹ against a 25 cm⁻¹ correction), the ring-skeleton modes do not — which tells the main plan which band families a learned model must carry and which it need not. Under its own rule the side project's cost branch closes on 15 October unless a pending test (P25, this week) licenses the last lever; what remains afterwards is a tests-only line of falsifiable questions, each with a losing condition written before the test.

**One question for the supervisor:** should this side project continue as that tests-only line beside the main plan (it costs desk time and no cluster time), or be closed at the 15 October review?

## What plan 06 is

A side study, opened on 12 September 2026, asking one question in two forms about the project's expensive step — the coupled-cluster correction Δ₂ to a DFT Hessian, measured as a deck of local-CC energies (K = 448 at benzene, ≈ 474 at naphthalene): **(M) is there a different mathematics that gives the same correction within the project's error budget, and (C) can the deck be made cheap enough that the pipeline runs many times?** It works only on data the main plan already has (the sealed benzene dry-run tensor, stored DFT Hessians of eight PAHs, run logs) and on desk mathematics; anything it finds enters the main plan only through that plan's dated decision record. One such transfer exists so far (decision 34 of 13 September: the substitution layer as an option of the main plan, conditional on the gradient cost); a second, P25, awaits its pre-registered test on naphthalene, whose input finishes on 16 September.

## What has been established (measured on the benzene stand-in tensor unless stated)

*The stand-in tensor:* the main plan's benzene dry run computed the difference between two DFT Hessians (BHHLYP − B3LYP, 6-31G*, psi4) as a rehearsal of the coupled-cluster correction — a matrix of the same shape, symmetry and rough size, available in full. Items 1–3 were measured on that surrogate; none of them yet rests on a real local-coupled-cluster deck, which exists only as the sealed benzene pilot and the naphthalene probe cells of the main plan.

1. **The correction is a symmetry-blocked, nearly dense object, and it is not local.** It has full rank (30 of 30); only 47 of its 435 off-diagonal mode pairs are symmetry-allowed; in real space its far atom-pair blocks are small in norm but not negligible for band positions — keeping 99 % of the Frobenius norm still moves bands by 14 cm⁻¹, and 74 of 78 atom-pair blocks are needed for 0.5 cm⁻¹ [X8]. Its share of the DFT Hessian *rises* with bond-graph distance (2.7 % on-atom → 6.5 % at three bonds; 19–26 % for ring C–C pairs) [X9]: the correction follows the π system, not the bonds. For the DFT Hessians of nine PAHs the far blocks are low-rank in norm (as the 2025 O1NumHess method assumes) but need 30–83 % of their rank at 0.5 cm⁻¹ [X15]. A method rule follows: sparsity or low rank must be tested on band positions, never on norms. (Two different objects are called "local" in this annex: here the *curvature* of the correction in real space, which reaches three bonds; in item 5 the MP2 *pair energies*, which decay within 3 Å. Both statements hold; they are not in tension.)
2. **Exact recovery from few Hessian–vector products.** Powell–Toint substitution on the symmetry pattern recovers Δ₂ exactly (to 10⁻¹⁶) from 6–7 products at benzene (measured on the stand-in tensor) and, by the count of the symmetry pattern alone, **9 products at naphthalene** — the size of the largest irreducible-representation block; no naphthalene correction existed to recover — i.e. 18 gradients against the 474-energy deck [X1c, X14]; the recovery theorems are machine-checked in Lean 4/Mathlib (no `sorry`). Measurement noise is magnified 1.4–1.5× [X1d]. With energies only, the products cost more than the deck; the route pays only with an analytic or automatic-differentiation gradient, and pays as soon as one gradient costs fewer than 26.3 energies at naphthalene (the one operative threshold, from X14; the 6/20 thresholds of the pre-registration and the 34–40 and 4.4 of the cost ladder belong to other levers or are superseded). The production local-CC code has no gradient; the AD route ran at the mean-field and MP2 levels and did not fit the laptop's memory at the coupled-cluster level (item 4).
3. **A free DFT ordering of the deck.** Ranking the symmetry-allowed pairs by the resonance denominator 1/|ω_i² − ω_j²| — the standard VPT2 resonance criterion, applied here to which harmonic couplings to measure first — selects 19 of 47 pairs for 0.5 cm⁻¹ at benzene (a perfect element-size prior: 17) [X10]; the substitution count on that pattern drops from 7 to 4 [X11]. Proposed to the main plan as an *ordering* the existing stopping rule judges, never a truncation (P25); its licence test is the naphthalene repeat, pre-registered.
4. **The gradient cost g, measured where it could be (14 September).** Every gradient-based saving hangs on g, the cost of one local-CC gradient in units of one energy; no paper prints it. The pre-registered measurement (M2a, PySCFAD 0.3.3, benzene, cc-pVDZ) gave **g = 2.84 m at the mean-field level and 3.20 m at MP2** — inside the automatic-differentiation constant — and **could not be run at the coupled-cluster level on the laptop**: the local-CCSD and (T) cells exceed its 25 GB (three requirements of the AD code were met one by one before that was certain; recorded). Consequence for the decision rule: its closing arm ("g unmeasurable on the laptop at cc-pVDZ") is the live one; the cost branch closes on 15 October unless P25 is licensed (below).

## What is and is not new

Every ingredient exists: nearsightedness and its decay theorems (Prodan & Kohn 2005; Benzi, Boito & Razouk 2013), sparse-Hessian recovery by graph colouring (Curtis–Powell–Reid 1974; Powell & Toint 1979; Coleman & Moré 1984), compressed-sensing Hessians of polyacenes (Sanders, Andrade & Aspuru-Guzik 2015), O(1)-gradient Hessians by off-diagonal low rank (Wang, Luo, Wang & Liu 2025), AD gradients of local CC (Zhang et al. 2024), the VPT2 resonance criterion. A citation chase over 873 works citing seventeen of these seeds (OpenAlex, 13 September) did not find the colouring literature entering chemistry, nor a coupled-cluster correction to a PAH Hessian recovered from few local-CC measurements — a search of titles and abstracts of the citing works, which is all it can say. Plan 06's own piece is the combination around that object, measured, with the band-level test and the finding that the correction is the long-range part of the problem. It has produced no new solver.

## Discipline

A dated decision rule (adopted 13 September; its text is the one in force, unchanged) closes the cost branch on 15 October 2026 if g > 26 or is unmeasurable on the laptop **and** P25 fails its test, and reviews the mathematics branch on 1 December 2026. As of 16 September the first condition holds; the expected verdict is closure of the cost branch unless P25 is licensed this week. Two tolerances appear in this annex on purpose: tests of whether a *representation* (sparse, low-rank, π-only) reproduces the correction itself are read at 0.5 cm⁻¹, a fifth of the main plan's per-family band-position budget, because a representation error adds to every other error; tests of whether a *cheaper measurement* lands within that budget are read at the budget itself (2.5 cm⁻¹ per family). Every experiment names the decision it serves before it runs; failed tests stay in the record with the sentence that failed them.

## Update, 14 September 2026 (the draft above is unchanged; this section records what the two days since it added or closed)

5. **Two directions of the mathematics branch met their pre-stated tests.** *S3 (a π-space DMRG anchor) is closed:* the π-valence CAS(6,6) carries only 9 % of the MP2 correlation curvature on benzene's C–C stretch (the share is the ratio of the two fitted curvatures of the correlation energy along the mode, π-CAS over MP2; a negative share, −48 % on the C–H out-of-plane mode, means the π-space curvature has the opposite sign to the full correlation curvature), so the ring-mode correction is whole-valence dynamic correlation, not a π problem [X6, run 14 September]. *S1's rate claim (conjecture T3, decay rate ∝ gap) survives without support:* the MP2 pair-energy decay length is 0.67 / 0.70 / 0.75 Å across benzene, naphthalene and pyrene while the B3LYP gap falls 44 % (6.80 → 3.84 eV); λ·gap moves by a factor 1.57, under the pre-stated 2, but a rate ∝ gap would have λ rise by 77 % and it rises by 12 % — the pair correlation is nearest-neighbour (2–6 % beyond 3 Å) and nearly gap-independent at this size [X7]. T3 stays a conjecture for the 1 December review — the pre-stated factor 2 was lenient, and the data lean against the claim; what closes T3 on 1 December is a fourth molecule (tetracene or pyrene's larger neighbour) whose λ·gap leaves the 1.57 band upward, or the same three molecules at the coupled-cluster level showing the same gap-independence.
6. **The formal route gained its second theorem.** Coleman & Moré's characterisation of symmetric colourings (Theorem 2.2, 1984: symmetrically consistent partition ⇔ proper colouring of the pattern graph with no two-coloured path of length three) is machine-checked in Lean 4/Mathlib (T1d, `symmValid_iff`, built 14 September, no `sorry`), beside T1a–T1c. What these certificates protect is one thing only: that the substitution count the cost branch quotes (9 products at naphthalene) rests on theorems whose statements were checked, so that the count cannot be wrong for a reason of algebra; they say nothing about spectra. Next in the named list: the combinatorial half of the decay theorems (T3a: a matrix respecting a graph has zero entries of its k-th power beyond graph distance k), then the ball count (T3b).
7. **The cost branch's largest levers turned out to lie in the main plan, not in plan 06.** Two plan-05 measurements of 14 September dominate the cost ladder (§5 of the ladder note): the local-CC price falls **11.6× m** from cc-pVTZ to cc-pVDZ at benzene and **10.0× m** at naphthalene (69 min against 11.5 h per energy at the tight thresholds, 15 September; memory 1.7 against 19.8 GB), with the basis step carried by MP2 and a beyond-MP2 increment (+7.9 / +1.0 / −6.0 cm⁻¹ on three benzene modes, m) whose transfer per band family is the pre-registered probe (its cc-pVTZ half runs 16–22 September); and patterns confined to a non-totally-symmetric irrep need one energy instead of a ± pair (the odd part of the energy along benzene's b₂u mode is ≤ 0.55 µE_h m at the deck's thresholds, against a curvature bias of 48–85 µE_h; at naphthalene, cc-pVDZ tight, the odd parts were ≤ 2.4 µE_h with the factory's slightly impure normal modes and ≤ 0.001 µE_h once the geometry is symmetrised and the modes projected onto their irreps, 15 September m — the prerequisite the main plan now enforces), which removes ≈ 38 % of naphthalene's deck (a count, e until the deck runs). If both are licensed, one naphthalene-class correction costs about 46 laptop-days (e: 291 energies at 3.8 h) — two to three desktop-weeks by the never-timed core-count rule, e — instead of a desktop-year, with no gradient. Plan 06's own contribution to the cost branch remains the recovery algebra (item 2, multiplying with the above if g is small) and the free ordering (item 3); its discipline (item "Discipline") is unchanged, and the 15 October criterion is read with that division of labour in mind.
8. **A per-family transfer measured (16 September).** Writing the stand-in correction in local internal coordinates and carrying one constant per local bond-pair type from benzene to naphthalene (X17, X18): the **C–H stretch** family transfers to **0.27 cm⁻¹ RMS m** (25 cm⁻¹ without transfer), the **C–C stretch does not** (17.8 of 41.2 cm⁻¹), the in-plane bends lie between (5.8 of 35.8; 6.4 of 22.0); the out-of-plane family has no type model yet. Under its pre-registered rule this is a loss for the internal-coordinate route as a way to the *whole* correction, and a per-family result the main plan uses: the σ-local families need no learned model to cross sizes, the ring-skeleton families do.
