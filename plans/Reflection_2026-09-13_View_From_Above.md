# Reflection 2026-09-13 — the view from above: would I do exactly what the repository says, and what has nobody here tried?

*Written at the user's request after reading the whole repository (three full-repository reads on 13 September; the summaries are in this session's record, the files they cite are the plans' own). This is a reflection, not a plan: nothing here changes plan 05 or plan 06, and no plan 07 is opened by it. Every "not found" below means "not found in what this project has read"; every lead marked (to verify) has not been checked against a source and must be before it is cited anywhere. House rules apply to this note as to every other.*

## 1. What the repository has learned, seen from a height

Six plans in three weeks converge on one insight that the first plan did not have: **the only new object is the difference between coupled cluster and DFT, and everything else is either available (DFT Hessians, intensities, anharmonic tiers, PAHdb) or unaffordable (whole surfaces, full decks, R6 by fragments).** Plan 05 built the instrument to measure that difference exactly (frozen local spaces, probing algebra, symmetry prior, checkpointed engine) and plan 06 measured, in two days, what the difference *is*:

- **It is not local.** Full rank; dense at the noise level in real space; its share of the mean-field Hessian *rises* with bond-graph distance (2.7 % on-atom → 6.5 % at three bonds, 19–26 % for ring C–C pairs); nothing is droppable at 0.5 cm⁻¹, not even in the mean field itself (X8, X9, X12, X15).
- **It is mode-specific physics, not family lore.** Within benzene the C–C stretch corrections run from −36 to +65 cm⁻¹; the 1357 cm⁻¹ B2u mode alone is −36. C–H stretches are uniform to 0.17 cm⁻¹ (T-1 self-test).
- **Symmetry is the only exact structure it has**, and it is worth a lot: 47 of 435 couplings at benzene, 141 of 1,128 at naphthalene; 18 gradients recover the whole naphthalene correction (X13, X14).
- **The price is in the decks, not the machines.** Full decks above naphthalene are out of reach on every route; tens of labelled molecules per year exist only up to naphthalene size, and only with thin decks or gradients (duration table, P26).
- **The residual after scaling is not predictable from simple descriptors** (Module 04: four models, R² ≤ 0.01 against the library's own error).
- **The laboratory decides less than one would like:** hot gas-phase records give band uncertainties of 8–16 cm⁻¹; no primary pair is decidable at 2–5 cm⁻¹; cold data exist for a handful of molecules (Module 03).

Put together: the project is trying to learn a **small, nonlocal, mode-specific quantity** (a CC−DFT harmonic correction of order 5 cm⁻¹ at benzene, Esposito 2024) with **expensive labels** that the **available laboratory truth can barely adjudicate**. That is not a reason to stop — the instrument is sound and R0–R1 will be real measurements — but it is the shape of the problem, and a bird sees three things about it that the ground plan does not emphasise.

## 2. Three observations from above

**(i) The binding constraint may be laboratory truth, not compute.** Every cost table in the repository is about coupled-cluster energies. But the goal sentence is "more accurate than the best prediction *wherever the laboratory data can decide it*", and Module 03 has shown how rarely they decide. A single new cold measurement of the right molecule (naphthalene or pyrene mid-IR, jet-cooled, at the resolution Lemmens' group reaches) would make more of the ladder scoreable than any amount of compute. The supervisor co-authored exactly that kind of measurement (Maltseva 2016). **This is the cheapest lever in the whole project and it appears nowhere as a lever** — only as "advice on sources" in the asks of §13. It should be a first-class item: what would one experiment cost, and which molecule and range would make R1–R2 decidable?

**(ii) The correction's nonlocality is a symptom, and symptoms are treated at the cause.** X9's rising ratio says the CC−DFT difference lives in the π system's response. Physically, that is the functional's error in π-conjugated systems (delocalisation, bond-length alternation; the Kekulé mode's −36 cm⁻¹ is the textbook case). A nonlocal *matrix* correction can come from a *local* error in the functional. Learning the matrix (P26's network) fights the nonlocality; correcting the functional would not have to. See §3, lead A.

**(iii) The astronomical use-case does not need what the plan is most careful about.** The framing forbids claims finer than 10 cm⁻¹ in astronomical language, and the plan's own headline gain is ≈ 5 cm⁻¹ on positions. What the identification problem (6.2/7.7/8.6/11.2 µm ratios) needs at least as much is **intensities and profiles** — where DFT's errors are larger, where the plan promises nothing, and where a free lunch is lying on the table (§3, lead G). This does not change the truth rungs; it changes what the reach product should carry.

## 3. Leads not in the repository, in order of my preference (each with a cheap first test and an honest risk)

**A. Learn the functional, not the matrix — a tuned-functional baseline the network must beat.** Fit *one or two parameters* of a range-separated hybrid (the range-separation ω, possibly the exact-exchange fraction) so that the DFT Hessian's per-mode diagonal reproduces the probed CC correction on benzene (R0's own deck; no new CC energies), then predict naphthalene's correction from the *tuned functional's* Hessian and compare with R1's probed diagonal. A functional is transferable by construction, size-consistent, charge-aware (cations for free), costs one DFT Hessian per molecule at any size, and its Hessian is exact — no probing, no network, no g. If it captures most of the CC correction across sizes, the reach problem is solved by DFT itself and the network becomes a small residual model; if it does not, the failure is informative (which modes resist, i.e. what is *not* a functional-shaped error). *First test:* desk-level today on the DFT−DFT stand-in (does a one-parameter interpolation between B3LYP and BHHLYP reproduce naphthalene's diagonal from benzene's? — trivially yes for that stand-in, so the real test needs the CC diagonal at R0 and R1: the R0 pilot and a 96-energy naphthalene diagonal at tight, ≈ 4 laptop-days). *Risk:* tuned hybrids are established for excitations and gaps (Baer–Kronik lineage, to verify) and for vibrational frequencies of PAHs I have not verified any prior work — a search before any claim. *Why it is not in the repo:* every plan corrected the *output* of DFT; none corrected DFT.

**B. Adaptive, cross-molecule deck design.** The deck is a fixed pattern per molecule with a stopping rule. In a numbers machine the right question is "which single energy, on which molecule, most reduces the uncertainty of the *model's* prediction on the classes we care about" — Bayesian experimental design with a symmetry-aware Gaussian-process prior over per-mode corrections across molecules (the Δ-learning and force-field-fitting communities do this; to verify the nearest work). It replaces "deck per molecule" by "one budget, many molecules, adaptively spent", and it is the natural home for P25's ordering and for X10's oracle gap (19 vs 17 pairs). *First test:* on the DFT−DFT stand-in corpus once the Module 05 factory has run a few hundred molecules (desktop-days). *Risk:* an infrastructure project; only worth it once T-2 shows transfer exists at all.

**C. Record the dipole at every probed geometry — intensities for the marginal price of zero.** Every energy point of every deck already has an SCF and, cheaply, an MP2 density; their dipoles at the displaced geometries are the ingredients of dipole derivatives, i.e. of intensity corrections along the probed modes. LNO-CCSD(T) dipoles need a response calculation and are not free, but the HF→MP2 intensity change along each mode *is*, and it tells whether correlation moves intensities where DFT is known to fail (the 6.2/7.7 µm ratio). *First test:* one flag in the probe scripts; a table nobody has for these molecules. *Risk:* none to the plan; the rule "positions scored, intensities reported" stays.

**D. Corrections in redundant internal coordinates: a transferable σ part and a stubborn π part.** Plan 04's motif transfer failed for *bands*; nobody transformed Δ₂ into bond-stretch and angle-bend force-constant corrections and asked which of *those* transfer. T-1's C–H stretch result (0.17 cm⁻¹ uniform) suggests the σ skeleton's corrections are transferable and the π ring's are not. If so, the network's job shrinks to the π part, and lead A's functional is the natural tool for exactly that part. *First test:* a B-matrix transform of the benzene stand-in tensor today; benzene→naphthalene when the BHHLYP half exists. *Risk:* redundancy makes internal-coordinate force constants non-unique; the test must be done on a fixed, declared coordinate set.

**E. The π problem as its own label generator (S3 sharpened into an embedding).** If X6 shows the ring correction to be mostly π, then for long acenes and flat flakes a π-active-space DMRG (Hachmann et al. reached (50,50)) embedded in the DFT σ frame could produce CC-quality π corrections at sizes local CC cannot afford — the user's "solve the cloud" made specific: not the whole molecule, the π subspace, as the *label generator for the large classes*. *First test:* X6, already in the queue. *Risk:* DMRG-in-DFT embedding for vibrational Hessians is, as far as this project has read, not established; a literature check first.

**F. Two-endpoint reach: small PAH ↔ graphene.** For very large flakes the interior converges to graphene; a periodic CC-quality (or embedded-cluster) correction of graphene's in-plane phonons at the Γ point would give the interior's correction once and for all, with the edges taken from small PAHs. Physically motivated, honest about what R6 is (interior + edge classes), and it replaces "extrapolate from C₂₄ to C₃₈₄" by "interpolate between two measured ends". *Risk:* periodic CC is expensive and young (to verify what exists for graphene phonons); a plan-07-sized idea, not a note.

**G. A cold measurement as a deliverable** — observation (i) turned into an ask: propose to the supervisor one jet-cooled or low-temperature gas-phase measurement (molecule, range, resolution) chosen so that R1 or R2 becomes decidable per family, and design the scoreboard around it. The project would then own one piece of laboratory truth instead of only borrowing. *Risk:* not in the student's hands; but it costs a page to ask.

Not proposed, and why: neural-network wavefunctions (cost far above Hessians), THC/low-rank (negative twice), voxel or field representations (plan 01's grave), motif transfer of bands (plan 04's), classical MD spectra (Mai 2025 owns it), a co-owned light–matter solver (plan 03's).

## 4. So: would I do exactly what the repository says?

For **R0–R1, yes, and soon**: the instrument is built, the seal discipline is right, F arrives tonight and g this week; nothing above replaces a measured naphthalene correction. For **the reach**, I would change the order of bets before building the network: run lead A as the first baseline (it is cheaper than the network and, if it works, makes the network small), add lead C for free, run lead D on the stand-ins now, and put lead G in front of the supervisor as a question. P26's network stays the right *shape* of the reach product; it should be trained to predict the *residual after the tuned functional*, not the whole correction. And I would state in the proposal, plainly, that laboratory decidability is a first-order risk with a first-order remedy.

If the user wants a plan 07, it is leads A + D (functional and σ/π split) as one idea plan beside 05 and 06, with the same protocol: a falsification test per direction, on data that exist, before any compute is spent.

Three things it changes in the text above, none applied here: (1) lead A's "to verify" is now verified — tuned range-separated hybrids are established (Baer–Kronik lineage), one paper tunes with vibrations in view (Tamblyn et al. 2014, and its zero-point energies are *worse* than B3LYP), and Körzdörfer et al. 2011 measured that the optimal ω of oligoacenes drifts almost linearly with length, so the transfer test proposed under A is the right test and the literature predicts where it fails; the cheap baseline A must beat is a double hybrid (Kesharwani, Brauer & Martin 2015: 10–12 cm⁻¹ harmonic RMSD against 5 at CCSD(T)/CBS), not a tuned hybrid. (2) Lead D has an ancestor: the scaled-quantum-mechanical force-field lineage (Pulay 1983; Rauhut & Pulay 1995; Baker, Jarzecki & Pulay 1998; Bock et al. 1990 on transfer between conjugated hydrocarbons). (3) Observation (i) is narrower than stated: rotationally resolved band origins of naphthalene's ν46 exist (Albert et al. 2011, room temperature; Pirali et al. 2013, jet-cooled) and belong in Module 03, while the cold FELIX data are ≈ 1 % broad; the ask under G is therefore a rotationally resolved jet measurement of the other strong bands, not another broadband scan. The nearest prior art for the *project's* central move (few local-CC energies correcting a cheap surface) is the Δ-ML line (Käser et al. 2021; Qu et al. 2021), which the novelty assessment must now name.
